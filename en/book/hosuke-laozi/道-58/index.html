The Book of Dao · Chapter Fourteen · The Thread of the Dao

English Translation

Looked at, and not seen — its name is the minute (微; MS A writes 聲). Listened for, and not heard — its name is the rarefied (希). Reached for, and not grasped — its name is the level (夷). These three cannot be probed to their end, and so, blended, they are one.

As for the one: above, it is not bright; below, it is not dark. Unending, unending — ah (尋尋呵) — it cannot be named, and returns home to thinglessness.

Call it the shape of no shape, the image of no thing — call it the flickering and dim (沕朢, read as 惚恍). Follow it, and you do not see its back; meet it, and you do not see its head.

Hold the Dao of today (執今之道) to steer the presence of today, and so to know the beginning of old: this is called the thread of the Dao (道紀).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The opening lines, 其下不物 through 是胃无狀之狀, and 執今之道 follow Manuscript A (MS A); 故緍而為一, 一者亓上不謬, the run from 无物之象 to 迎而不見亓首, and the last line are damaged in MS A and supplied from Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 視之而弗見名之曰聲: Follows MS A. For 聲 the Jicheng traces a graph with 微 over 耳, treats it as a miswriting and reads it as 微 "minute"; in Gao Ming's photograph it looks like 殸 over 耳. On the plate the lower part is clearly 耳 but the upper part is blurred, and 殸 and 微 cannot be told apart, so 聲 is retained. MS B reads □之曰微, with 命 lost and 微 in a variant form. Wang Bi has 視之不見名曰夷, assigning 夷 to sight and 微 to touch — the reverse of the silk text.
  • 聽之而弗聞名之曰希: Follows MS A; MS B writes 命 for 名 here and at its two later occurrences. Wang Bi has 聽之不聞名曰希.
  • 捪之而弗得名之曰夷: Follows MS A; MS B also has 捪 "to stroke", preceded by a graph first miswritten as 聽 and cancelled by inking over its 心 component. Wang Bi has 搏之不得名曰微. (Jicheng IV, p. 208, n. 18)
  • 三者不可至計: The manuscripts agree; the Jicheng reads 至計 as 致詰 "to probe to the end". Wang Bi has 此三者，不可致詰.
  • 故緍而為一: Follows MS B. MS A's first graph, formerly transcribed 園, is described by the Jicheng as 束 within 囗; the 1980 editors identified it with the Shuowen radical 㯻, read as 梱, which the Jicheng doubts, citing the alternative view that it is a variant of 困 read as 混 "to blend". The following three graphs are lost. Wang Bi has 故混而為一. (Jicheng IV, p. 48, n. 51)
  • 一者亓上不謬: Follows MS B. In MS A the 一 stood at the foot of the preceding column and is lost; only its repetition mark survives at the head of the next column, and the original editors read it as 一 on the model of MS B. MS A reads 其上不攸. Wang Bi has 其上不皦. (Jicheng IV, p. 48, n. 52)
  • 其下不物尋尋呵: Follows MS A, whose 物 the Jicheng reads as 忽; MS B reads 亓下不忽, its 忽 printed with the heart radical beside 勿. Wang Bi has 其下不昧, lacks 尋尋呵, and continues 繩繩不可名.
  • 不可名也: Follows MS A; MS B reads 不可命也.
  • 无物之象 / 是胃沕朢: MS A breaks off after 无物之; the lines follow MS B. The Jicheng reads 沕朢 as 惚恍 "flickering and dim". Wang Bi has 是謂惚恍.
  • 隋而不見亓後 / 迎而不見亓首: Follows MS B. In MS A the first line is wholly lost and the second survives only as 而不見其首; the graph above 而 was restored as 迎 in the 1980 edition, but the Jicheng, unable to identify its traces, marks it as illegible. The silk text names the back first and the head second; Wang Bi reverses the order, 迎之不見其首，隨之不見其後, and has 之 for 而. (Jicheng IV, p. 48, n. 56)
  • 執今之道 / 以御今之有: The manuscripts agree; Wang Bi has 執古之道 "hold the Dao of old".
  • 以知古始 / 是胃道紀: Both manuscripts have 以知; Wang Bi has 能知古始. 道紀 is lost in MS A and supplied from MS B.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The Dao as a thing is beyond sight and hearing, beyond the grasping hand, and so Laozi, having no better way, gives it three names: the minute, the rarefied, the level (so manuscript B; manuscript A writes 聲, "sound," for the minute — sitting oddly with sight, and probably an error, which the Jicheng takes as a miswritten graph and reads as 微). "Looked at and not seen, listened for and not heard": the Dao stands outside form and name, beyond what the private ear and eye can sound. The Zhuangzi has the voice called No-Beginning say: "the Dao cannot be heard — what is heard is not it; the Dao cannot be seen — what is seen is not it; the Dao cannot be spoken — what is spoken is not it." Just this.

The silk text writes 捪之而弗得, "reached for and not grasped," where Wang Bi has 搏之不得, "grappled and not gotten": both pass, but the silk text's 捪, with its hand-sign, means stroking and following, and so shows the Dao as not far from men at all. "These three cannot be probed to their end" — Wang Bi writes 致詰, "questioned to the limit": near in sense, but 至計, "reckoned to the end," better shows the Dao standing past all reckoning. Three names given, and none to be probed — so, blended, they are one. And "blended into one" is not three compounded into one: the Dao by root refuses names, standing above the distinctions of the three. What the senses divide is the difference of the ten thousand things; what the Dao holds whole is their oneness. Laozi neither dotes on the old nor flaunts the new — every time is the Dao's time, and the Dao, unsayable, cannot be interrogated by names.

"As for the one: above, not bright; below, not dark" (manuscript A, 其上不攸，其下不物; manuscript B has 亓上不謬，亓下不忽) — the sense of the flickering-and-dim is complete. 攸 probably borrows for 皦, "bright." 物 the Jicheng reads as 忽 (manuscript B in fact writes 忽), and 忽 is much the same as 昧, "dark," which is what Wang Bi writes. Above, it is not bright-clear; below, not dark-dim: past the pair of light and dark, one undivided whole. "Follow it, and you do not see its back; meet it, and you do not see its head" (manuscript B; manuscript A keeps only "… and you do not see its head"; the silk text speaks first of the back, then of the head, the reverse of Wang Bi's order). The silk text writes 而不見 where Wang Bi writes 之不見 — the silk text's wording better shows the Dao without head or tail. The Great Commentary: "the spirit has no direction; the Change has no body" — one sense.

And then the distinction of holding-the-present against holding-the-past: the silk text reads 執今之道，以御今之有 — "hold the Dao of today to steer the presence of today" — where the Wang Bi recension reads 執古之道, "hold the ancient Dao." Each has its ground. The silk text's 无物 is the Daoists' older writing. "And so to know the beginning of old (以知古始; Wang Bi has 能知, "can know"): this is called the thread of the Dao." The thread of the Dao is the Dao's guiding cord — not a record of history but the sense of the Dao threading through time. Knowing the beginning of old is how one passes through to today and holds today: the Dao stands beyond time, and stands inside time — and its use never ends.
