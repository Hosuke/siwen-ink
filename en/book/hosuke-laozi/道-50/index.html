The Book of Dao · Chapter Six · The Valley Spirit

English Translation

The valley spirit (浴神, read 谷神) does not die — call it the dark female (xuanpin, 玄牝).

The gate of the dark female — call it the root of heaven and earth.

Gossamer, gossamer — ah (綿綿呵) — as if it were there. Use it, and it never runs out (不堇).

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

Twenty-seven words, and the whole purport of the Book of Dao — the deep source of the world's coming-to-be. "The valley spirit does not die" and "the bathing spirit does not die" come to the same: 谷 is the name of what is empty and so can receive; 浴 is bathing one's De, washing the heart. Both say the Dao's substance is open like a valley, its spirit-work unfathomable, its labor of birthing things never once at rest. An old comment runs: "in the valley's center there is no valley — no form, no shadow, no opposing, no crossing; dwelling low, unmoved; keeping still, unworn." Well said. And why does the spirit not die? Because the Dao itself is unborn and undying. "The spirit got the One and was numinous" — lose the One and it gives out: this is why the valley spirit does not die.

"Call it the dark female": 玄 is the name of the dim and deep; 牝 is mother. The gate of the dark female is what the ten thousand transformations come out of — the root of heaven and earth. The chapter on heaven-long-earth-lasting says, "heaven and earth last because they do not live for themselves": not self-living, and the ten thousand things live by them — the very point of the dark female.

"Gossamer, gossamer — ah — as if it were there" — the silk text breathes one extra 呵, picturing the faintness, the unhurriedness. "Use it, and it never runs out": the silk text writes 堇, Wang Bi 勤 — old graphs passing for each other, both saying use cannot exhaust it. An old commentary: "the Dao's birthing of things has no hour of depletion; the ten thousand things lean on it and live." The Dao's being: as if gone, as if there — nothing can name it; the Dao's use: never brimming, never dry — nothing can sound it. Every graph with a shape is too poor to write the Dao; only unsettled words like "empty," "hollow," "as if there," "gossamer" come near. This is Laozi saying the yes of the Dao in the grammar of no.

The Changes says of the hexagram Fu ䷗ (Return), "in return — do we not see the heart of heaven and earth?" — the first stirring of yang, the mechanism of birth showing itself. Heaven-and-earth's heart and the dark female's gate: different names, one thing — the wellspring of the living mechanism. The Confucians speak of heaven-and-earth's heart; the Daoists, of the dark female's gate — not two things. Valley-spirit is the Dao's substance; dark-female is the Dao's function; substance and function one source, and no second Dao. The world gallops after contrivance, hustles for name and gain, never knowing that inside this very body there is an undying spirit. "Water benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still" — the subtle working of exactly that undying spirit. To know it — what else but to come back to the dark female's gate, and keep the emptiness that keeps the spirit whole?
