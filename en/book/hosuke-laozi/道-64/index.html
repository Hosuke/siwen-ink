The Book of Dao · Chapter Twenty · Fed by the Mother

English Translation

Cut off learning — and no sorrow. A courteous "yes" and a curt reply — how far apart are they? Beautiful and ugly — how far apart are they?

What people fear — one too cannot fail to fear people.

Vast, ah (朢呵) — it has no end yet.

The crowd is jubilant (巸巸), as if feasting at the great sacrifice, as if climbing the terrace in spring.

I alone am still (泊) and signless, like the newborn (嬰兒) that has not yet laughed. Weary, ah — as if with nowhere to go home to.

Everyone has more than enough; I alone am left out. Mine is a fool's heart — murky, murky, ah. The worldly (鬻人) are bright-bright; I alone seem dim. The worldly are sharp-sharp; I alone am muddled-muddled, ah.

Indistinct, ah (忽呵) — it is like the sea. Vast, ah — as if nowhere to rest.

Everyone has a purpose; I alone am stubborn and rustic (門□以鄙: 門 a stray graph, the damaged one read as 頑; MS A ends 以悝, "yet inwardly attuned"). I wish to be unlike others — and prize being fed by the mother (貴食母).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Both manuscripts are damaged in this chapter; the text follows Manuscript A (MS A) where it survives and is supplied from Manuscript B (MS B) where it does not. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng). In the Dao section MS A writes 其, MS B 亓.

  • 絶學无憂唯與呵: Follows MS B. In MS A 絕學無 is lost, while 憂 is read directly by the Jicheng; MS A writes 訶 for 呵. In Wang Bi 絕學無憂 opens chapter 20, followed by 唯之與阿.
  • 其相去幾何 / 美與惡 / 其相去何若: Follows MS A; MS B writes 亓 for 其 and 亞 for 惡, read as 惡. Wang Bi has 相去幾何, 善之與惡 and 相去若何: where the silk text pairs "beautiful" (美) with ugly, Wang Bi has "good" (善).
  • 人之所畏 / 亦不可以不畏人: In MS A 所畏 was bracketed as a restoration in the 1980 edition; the Jicheng reads it directly from a newly joined fragment at the foot of cols. 128–129. From 亦不 on MS A is lost and the text follows MS B. The 1980 editors suspected MS B's final 人 of being an interpolation; the Jicheng holds that it is genuine and adds 人 to its restoration of MS A as well. Wang Bi has 人之所畏，不可不畏, which changes the sense: "what others fear one must fear", as against "one too cannot but fear others". (Jicheng IV, p. 51, n. 75; p. 209, n. 29)
  • 朢呵亓未央才: Lost in MS A; follows MS B. 朢 is read as 恍 "dim, indistinct" and 才 as 哉. Wang Bi has 荒兮其未央哉.
  • 眾人巸巸若鄉於大牢 / 而春登臺: Follows MS A; MS B agrees. 鄉 is read as 饗 "to feast". Wang Bi has 如享太牢，如春登臺.
  • 我泊焉未兆: Follows MS A. The 1980 edition read 佻（兆）; the Jicheng, taking the apparent 人 on the left to be part of 兆 itself, provisionally reads 兆. MS B has 我博焉未𠧞: 博 is read as 泊 "still", and 𠧞, drawn as two 卜 back to back about a central stroke, was misread by the 1980 editors as 挑; the Jicheng follows Qiu Xigui in reading it as 兆 "sign, omen". Wang Bi has 我獨泊兮其未兆. (Jicheng IV, p. 51, n. 76; p. 209, n. 30)
  • 若嬰兒未咳: MS A's 嬰, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is read directly by the Jicheng from a newly joined fragment; the following 兒未咳 is lost and supplied from MS B. Wang Bi has 如嬰兒之未孩. (Jicheng IV, p. 51, n. 77)
  • 累呵 / 佁无所歸: 累呵 follows MS A; MS B writes 纍呵. For 佁无所歸 MS A has 如□□□ and the text follows MS B, whose 佁 is read as 似 "as if"; MS A has 如 instead. Wang Bi has 儽儽兮若無所歸.
  • 眾人皆又余 / 我獨遺: 眾人皆又余 follows MS B (又余 read as 有餘); MS A has □□皆有餘. 我獨遺 follows MS A. MS B, having copied 我, skipped on to the next clause 愚人之心 and so omitted three graphs, which the Jicheng marks as 獨遺我. Wang Bi has 眾人皆有餘，而我獨若遺. (Jicheng IV, p. 209, n. 31)
  • 我禺人之心也惷惷呵: Follows MS A, with 禺 read as 愚 "foolish"; MS B reads 我愚人之心也湷湷呵. Wang Bi has 我愚人之心也哉！沌沌兮.
  • 鬻人昭昭 / 我獨若𨴽呵鬻人察察: Lost in MS A, which preserves only 閩呵鬻人蔡蔡; the text follows MS B. For 𨴽 the Jicheng prints 門 enclosing 口 over 心, read as 昏 "dim"; Gao Ming's transcription gives 閩, and on the plate a single stroke runs through the enclosure, close to the 虫 element, so the form cannot be settled. Wang Bi has 俗人昭昭，我獨昏昏；俗人察察.
  • 我獨閩閩呵忽呵 / 亓若海朢呵: The first follows MS A, its 閩閩 read as 閔閔 "muddled"; MS B writes 沕 for 忽. In the second, 海 is lost in MS A and the line follows MS B. Wang Bi has 我獨悶悶。澹兮其若海.
  • 其若无所止: Follows MS A; MS B reads 若无所止, without 亓. Wang Bi has 飂兮若無止.
  • 眾人皆有以 / 我獨門□以鄙: In MS A 眾 and 人皆 were bracketed as restorations in 1980; the Jicheng reads them directly from a fragment newly joined by Chen Jian. The rest is lost in MS A and the text follows MS B. The 1980 editors took MS B's 門 for a cancelled graph miscopied from 閩閩 above; since it bears no deletion mark, the Jicheng treats it as an interpolated graph. The next graph has lost its left half and keeps 元 on the right; it is read as 頑 "obstinate". Gao Ming's transcription has a single graph, 閩; on the plate 門 and 元 each occupy only half a graph's height, so they may be one graph, but the matter cannot be settled. MS A reads □□□以悝, with 悝 read as 俚 "rustic". Wang Bi has 而我獨頑似鄙. (Jicheng IV, p. 51, n. 80; p. 209, n. 35)
  • 吾欲獨異於人 / 而貴食母: The manuscripts agree. MS A's 吾 is given as 我 in Gao Ming's transcription; the Jicheng reads 吾. Wang Bi has 我獨異於人, without 欲 "wish".

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The crux of this chapter is what makes the man of the Dao unlike the crowd. It opens with "yes sir" and "how dare you," "beautiful" and "ugly" — all of them the looks of distinction. 唯 is the agreeable answer; 訶 is the surly one. The world gallops through the arena of right and wrong, and even in answering and accepting it counts the longer and shorter — the blindness of the age's learning. The silk text's "what people fear — one too cannot fail to fear people" — a line the Wang Bi text's sense lacks. Fear is what all men share; to be able to fear others is how the Dao goes everywhere. The Guanyinzi: "the Dao has no men in it, and the sage does not see that A is the Dao and B is not" — that stands beyond fearing and being feared.

"Vast, ah — it has no end yet" (manuscript B; 才 is read as 哉; manuscript A is lost here) — a variant of "wild, ah, without end." Wild is vastness; 朢 is gazing far: both say the Dao has no shore. The Dao fills and surrounds, wrapping the ten thousand there-ares — broad without bank, deep without bottom: just this.

"The crowd is jubilant (巸巸), as if feasting at the great sacrifice, and climbing the terrace in spring" — Wang Bi writes 熙熙，如享太牢，如春登臺. 熙熙 shows the crowd's delighted pressing-forward. And the silk text's 湷湷 against 沌沌: 湷 has the water-sign — the Dao's body muddled like turbid water, the deeper image. 蔡蔡 against 察察 likewise carries its own sense and must not be forced into the modern meaning.

"I alone am still and signless, like an infant that has not yet laughed": 兆 is the first crack of omen; signless is the chaos before any crack shows. "Mine is a fool's heart" — no true folly: the sage's knowing reaches the ten thousand things, and he abides as if dim — the sense of great wisdom looking like folly. The worldly are bright-bright; I alone seem dim. The worldly are sharp-sharp; I alone am muddled-muddled. Sharp-sharp is the picking examiner; muddled-muddled is the unspiced whole. The sage wins by the unspiced whole, not by preening his sharpness.

"Flickering, ah — it is like the sea": the image of the Dao's body, vast without shore. The sage's heart is open without margin — like the sea taking the hundred streams and refusing none.

"Everyone has a purpose; I alone am stubborn yet inwardly attuned": 頑 is lost in manuscript A, and manuscript B writes 門□ — 門 a stray, the next graph keeping only its right side 元, read as 頑; the silk text writes 以悝 where Wang Bi has 似鄙. 悝 is attunement (諧); 頑 is stubborn unshapedness — the silk text's phrase shows the sage's inward self-keeping, stronger than "as if rustic."

The close: "I wish to be unlike others — and prize being fed by the mother." 食母 is taking the Dao for the mother, drawing one's nourishment from the Dao. The silk text writes 吾 where Wang Bi writes 我 — the older breath. The Wenzi: "to hear and then know is sagacity": the sage hears the Dao and walks it, and so can come near the Dao, returning to the root and to what is ordained. The Great Learning: "know where to stop, and only then steadiness; steadied, then stillness" — being still and signless is exactly the work of knowing-where-to-stop; prizing the mother's feeding is exactly the proof of having gotten it.

"The highest good: governing as water — water benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still": this is what comes near to the root of the Dao. In dwelling, good at the ground; in heart, the deep pool; in giving, humaneness; in speech, trust; in works, ability; in moving, the season — the sage patterns on these, still and desireless, returning to the root and to what is ordained. The sage has no self; the Dao has no self — that is how he is unlike others, while in truth not unlike at all.

In sum, the chapter's great point is the parting of crowd and sage. The crowd gallops after outer things — bright-bright, sharp-sharp, preening on having more than enough; the sage returns to the root within — murky, dim, as if of no use at all. The world chases the outside; the cultivator returns to the root. The world prizes things; the sage prizes being fed by the mother. This is the shared truth of all ages, the fine word of sages. Let the student be still and desireless, returning to the root and to what is ordained, and the De of governing as water can be had sitting down.
