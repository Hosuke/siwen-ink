The Book of De · Chapter Twenty-nine · Rivers and Seas

English Translation

That rivers and seas can be kings of the hundred valley-streams (浴) is because they are good at lying low, and so can be kings of the hundred streams.

Hence the sage, wishing to be above the people, must with his words place himself below them; wishing to be before the people, must with his person place himself behind them. So he stands in front, and the people come to no harm; he stands above, and the people feel no weight. All-under-heaven gladly pushes him forward and never tires. Is it not because he does not dispute (无諍), and so no one in all-under-heaven can dispute with him?

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

That rivers and seas can be kings of the hundred valley-streams is because they are good at lying low. The silk text writes 浴: water at the left, the image of a bathing-fullness at the right — passing for 谷, the valley. Water's nature runs downward; the ravines and brooks race home to it. Rivers and seas sit below all the streams, so they can contain them and complete their greatness. "Water benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still": just this.

The sage embodies this Dao to steer all-under-heaven. Wishing to be above the people, he must with his words place himself below them; wishing to be before the people, he must with his person place himself behind them. Thus he stands in front and the people come to no harm; stands above and the people feel no weight; and all-under-heaven gladly pushes him forward and never tires. Silk manuscript A says 非以亓无諍與, where manuscript B and the Wang Bi recension write 爭. 諍 is straight speech that debates right and wrong; 爭 is the grappling and seizing of horns locked. What Laozi slights is not the forthright 諍 but the grappling 爭. Yet 无諍 runs finer than 不爭: it is not only to forgo competing with things: it is to house not even the thought of verbal dispute. "To take men by being good at lying low, never crossing things: the utmost of De" — is this not the very point? And "no one in all-under-heaven can dispute with him": not that I leave no room for dispute: there is simply no ground left under heaven from which to dispute him.

Heaven is long and earth lasting because they do not live for themselves; the sage puts his person behind and his person is in front, sets his person aside and his person endures. Water is the same: good at lying low, and the hundred streams come home; the sage good at lying low, and all-under-heaven comes home: one principle. This age, where the racing is fiercer than ever, runs the whole way backward — those who contend for the top end up below; those who contend to be first end up behind; those who contend for profit end up losing. Only he who is willing to dwell low, willing to put his person behind, can keep himself whole and receive the homecoming of all-under-heaven. This is Laozi's unchanging law.
