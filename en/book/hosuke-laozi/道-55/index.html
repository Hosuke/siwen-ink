The Book of Dao · Chapter Eleven · The Dark Center

English Translation

Thirty spokes share one hub (轂): where it is empty (當亓无) — there is the cart's use.

Kneaded clay (然埴, 然 read as 埏) makes a vessel: where it is empty — there is the vessel's use.

A door and a window (戶牖) cut out: where they are empty — there is the room's use.

Hence: presence (有) gives the advantage; absence (无) gives the use.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The first line, 有埴器之用也 and 鑿戶牖 are damaged in Manuscript A (MS A) and are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B); the rest follows MS A. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 卅楅同一轂: MS A preserves only 卅; the rest follows MS B. 卅 is read as 三十 "thirty", 楅 as 輻 "spoke". Wang Bi has 三十輻共一轂.
  • 當亓无 / 有車之用也: MS A reads □其无，有□之用□, with 當, 車 and 也 lost; the lines follow MS B. MS B writes 亓 where MS A writes 其, here and in the two later instances of 當其无. Wang Bi lacks the final 也.
  • 然埴為器: Follows MS A, whose graph is 然, read by the Jicheng as 埏 "to knead"; Gao Ming's transcription gives 撚. MS B reads 𡑋埴而為器, with an extra 而; for its first graph the Jicheng prints 撚 with the hand radical, but the left side is effaced on the color plate and cannot be decided between 扌 and 土, so the earlier transcription with 土 is retained. Wang Bi has 埏埴以為器.
  • 當其无 / 有埴器之用也: Follows MS A; 之用也 is lost in MS A and supplied from MS B. Both manuscripts have 埴器 "clay vessel"; Wang Bi has 有器之用.
  • 鑿戶牖: Lost in MS A and supplied from MS B. Wang Bi reads 鑿戶牖以為室 "cut doors and windows to make a room"; MS B and the Peking University manuscript lack 以為室, and although MS A is damaged here, the Jicheng infers from the number of missing graphs that it too lacked them. (Jicheng IV, p. 208, n. 14)
  • 當其无 / 有室之用也: Follows MS A; 室 is lost in MS A and supplied from MS B.
  • 故有之以為利 / 无之以為用: The manuscripts agree, as does Wang Bi.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

This chapter takes three vessels to show how presence and absence complete each other — and comes home to the true function of non-doing (无為).

A cart's thirty spokes gather at one hub: it is exactly at the empty place that the cart has its use. The spokes are the solid; the void at the hub's center is emptiness. The solid gives the shape; emptiness gives the work — the power of turning lies not in the spokes but in the hub's hollow. Kneaded clay makes a vessel: again, exactly at the empty place the vessel has its use. The walls are the solid; the hollow belly is emptiness. The solid forms the vessel; emptiness holds the goods — the power of containing lies not in the wall but in the vessel's hollow. A door and a window cut out make a room: again, at the empty place the room has its use. The walls are the solid; the openings are emptiness. The solid stands the room up; emptiness lets the light through — the power of dwelling lies not in the wall but in the room's hollow.

Three vessels, one law: presence gives the shape, absence gives the use — neither can be dropped. But the world sees only the presence, never the absence, and so loses the use. The Zhuangzi's "In the Human World" says: "everyone knows the use of the useful; no one knows the use of the useless" — exactly this. Heshang Gong: "the vessel is hollow inside, and so it can receive and hold things." The world chases presence and drops absence, chases form and slights the empty — like galloping around the hunting field and losing the hollow one always had. The Kun hexagram ䷁ speaks of earth's thick De carrying all things — it carries them by an empty middle — answering the Daoist function of empty absence.

Collating the two texts: the silk text writes 卅楅同一轂 and 當亓无 (manuscript B; manuscript A has 當其无), Wang Bi 三十輻共一轂 and 當其無. 卅 is the short form of thirty; 亓 is the old graph for 其; 同一 and 共一 are one sense; and the silk text closes each clause with 也 where Wang Bi drops it — small variants, no difference of sense. On the whole, the silk text is nearer the ancient and plain; Wang Bi is tidier and smoother.

The crux of the chapter: absence is not mere emptiness — it is the root of what makes things work. Whatever has a function owes it to its empty part; lose that emptiness and the thing's use cannot stand. Laozi's lone seeing is exactly here: everyone knows to chase presence — and no one knows that absence's work is greater than presence's.
