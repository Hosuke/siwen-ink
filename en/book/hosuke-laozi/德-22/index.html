The Book of De · Chapter Twenty-two · Long Life

English Translation

For governing men and serving heaven, nothing compares with husbandry (嗇).

Only the husbanding man submits early (蚤服). Early submission means heavily storing De; heavily storing De, nothing is unconquered; nothing unconquered, and none knows his limit; his limit unknown, he can hold the state. With the mother by which a state is held, he can last long.

This is called deep roots and a firm taproot — the way of long life and lasting sight.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The De of husbandry is subtle in its working and vast in its use. Heshang Gong glosses: "嗇 is to cherish" — he who governs men cherishes them as children; he who serves heaven patterns on the Dao's self-so; both take husbandry as root. But 嗇 is not miserliness: it is the sense of gathering and storing — the art of cherishing one's essence and spirit, husbanding one's wits. Husbandry stores within; frugality merely trims without. Outward trimming runs out in time; inward husbandry is never exhausted. The two senses must not be confused.

The silk text writes 蚤服; Wang Bi writes 早服. Yaomiao ventures: 蚤 can be read for the leap — moving before the event; 早 is merely the early hour. The graphs interchanged of old, but 蚤服 carries more: the husbanding man springs to the Dao's service, not waiting to be cornered before he seeks it, where 早 says no more than "not late." Heshang Gong: "早 is first; 服 is to get" — getting the Dao first, which answers the Great Learning's "the reaching of knowledge lies in the investigation of things."

"Heavily storing De" is no piling of deed on deed; it is an unbroken, spring-fed store. Husbandry lays it in; De accumulates: stored within, amassed in the invisible. So 重 is not repetition's "again and again" but the heaviness of depth.

"Nothing unconquered, and none knows his limit": the silk text's 亓 points back at the power of nothing-unconquered; Wang Bi's 其 passes, but the breath scatters. An enemy exists only where comparison exists; where none can stand against, there is nothing to measure by, and so the limit cannot be known. No boast — the innermost ground of self-mastery: he who has conquered himself has no enemy left in the heart, hence nothing unconquered.

"The mother of holding the state": 母 is the root. The Dao is the root of founding a state. The Wenzi: "the sage holds the hollow and carries harmony; without stepping from the hall he travels the four seas" — the lord who keeps the Dao by husbandry finds the ten thousand things transforming themselves; no contrivance, and all-under-heaven comes straight. Husbandry → early submission → heavy-stored De → nothing unconquered → the state's mother → lasting long: five steps in file, inside moving outward, rank on rank, as a tree has root and taproot.

The close: "deep roots and a firm taproot, the way of long life and lasting sight" — not the chase after deathlessness, but the refusal to rot from within. Deep roots: the inner strength complete in itself. A firm base: what the outside cannot shake. Like pine and cypress in frost and snow — not that they feel no cold, but that the cold cannot enter. Take drugs without keeping the spirit, and nothing is solved.

The way of husbandry: its use is now; its proof is far. The world knows the profit of doing and not the subtlety of non-doing; knows the achievements of exertion and not the way of sparing the spirit. Husband, and essence and spirit are not spent: subtle, dark-reaching, joined to the true of the Dao. "Water benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still": husbandry is like that — not contending yet good at winning, doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone. This is why he whose held De is thick compares with a naked babe.
