The Book of Dao · Chapter Twenty-five · Undivided at Its Making

English Translation

There is a thing, undivided at its making (有物昆成), born before heaven and earth.

Woven, ah, wound round, ah (繡呵繆呵) — it stands alone and does not change. It can be the mother of heaven and earth.

I do not know its name: I call it, by courtesy name, the Dao (字之曰道); and, forced to give it a name, I call it the Great (大).

The Great is called casting-forth (筮); casting-forth is called far; far is called turning-back (反).

The Dao is great; heaven is great; earth is great; and man too is great.

In the realm there are four greats — and the king holds one place among them.

Man patterns on earth; earth patterns on heaven; heaven patterns on the Dao; the Dao patterns on the self-so (道法自然).

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

This chapter gives the most complete account of the Dao's substance and function in the whole book; a student must not skim it.

The silk text writes 有物昆成, "a thing undivided at its making," where the Wang Bi recension writes 混成. 昆 is undivided wholeness — the time before the Dao was cut open, when the ten thousand things were one lump with no seam. 混 already smacks of mixture; 昆 is simply what it is of itself. "Undivided at its making" shows the Dao born before heaven and earth, whole of itself, not pieced together from anything. The Great Ancestral Teacher says: "the Dao has feeling and faithfulness, no doing and no form; its own root, its own stock — before there was heaven and earth, from of old it stood firm." Just this.

Next, 繡呵繆呵, "woven, ah, wound round, ah," where Wang Bi has 寂兮寥兮, "silent, ah, empty, ah." Silent-and-empty is the look of vacant stillness; woven-and-wound is the look of threads interlaced — different faces, both saying the Dao cannot be sought by form or name. 繡 like silk woven through silk, 繆 like cord coiled on cord: through all-under-heaven the Dao runs, nowhere it does not reach, nothing it does not enter — interwoven, coursing round, its use is vast.

"I call it, by courtesy name, the Dao; forced to give it a name, I call it the Great" — the sage naming under protest. The book's first chapter says, "the Dao that can be spoken of is not the constant Dao": he forces the courtesy name, forces the name Great — and this is no arbitrary invention. Every name is borrowed; let the reader get the meaning and forget the words.

"The Great is called casting-forth (筮); casting-forth is called far; far is called turning-back." The Wang Bi text puts 逝 in place of 筮: "the Great is called passing; passing is called far; far is called turning-back." 筮 is the diviner's work — casting the yarrow, pushing the reckoning forth; 逝, passing, also passes as a reading. For the Dao to be Great it must go out; going out, it goes far; going far, it turns back — coursing round without fail, ending and so beginning. The Book of Changes: "no level that does not tilt; no going that does not return" — the law is here.

Wang Bi writes 域中有四大, "in the realm there are four greats," where the silk text writes 國中, "in the state." The realm is boundless; the state has borders — for the Dao's sake, the realm is the better. But the point of the four greats is one: the Dao is great, heaven is great, earth is great, and man too is great. The king is mankind's representative: that man can stand level with heaven-and-earth's greatness — only by the king as its representative.

"Man patterns on earth; earth patterns on heaven; heaven patterns on the Dao; the Dao patterns on the self-so" — the order of the four patternings. Man patterns on earth, taking its thick De that favors none; earth patterns on heaven, taking its covering-and-bearing that favors none; heaven patterns on the Dao, taking its accomplishing without ruling; and the Dao patterns on the self-so — not "nature" as the natural world, but the so-of-itself: that by which the Dao is what it is. Even the Dao has something it patterns on — the self-so is the Dao of the Dao. Here is the eye of the chapter.

The Dao in all-under-heaven: great, far, wrapping the cosmos — and coming home, always, to the self-so. The sage knows the turning-back; the hundred clans use it daily and never know. Let the student gather this law of the circle, and the point of the Dao's birthing heaven and earth is more than half understood.
