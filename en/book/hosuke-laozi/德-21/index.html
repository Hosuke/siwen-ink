The Book of De · Chapter Twenty-one · Being Straight

English Translation

Its rule: muffled and mild (閔閔); its people: thick and true (屯屯). Its rule: sharp and searching (竊竊, read as 察察); its state: split and splintered (夬夬).

Calamity — what fortune leans on; fortune — where calamity crouches. Who knows where it ends?

It has no fixed straightness (正): the straight turns strange again; the good turns uncanny again (妖, dropped by the copyist, is supplied from the received text).

Long indeed have men been lost in this.

Therefore: square, but cutting no one; embracing (兼), but piercing no one; straight, but binding no one (紲); shining, but dazzling no one.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Manuscript A (MS A) is lost from 孰 onward: the first line combines both manuscripts, 𢢸福之所倚 and 福禍之所伏 follow MS A, and everything from 孰知亓極 on follows Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 亓正閔閔亓民屯屯亓正竊竊亓邦夬夬: Combines both manuscripts. The opening of MS A is lost except for the repetition mark after the last graph of its second clause. The original editors restored it as 其政閔閔，其邦屯屯; the Jicheng changes 邦 to 民 after MS B and the Peking University manuscript, and replaces the restored 屯屯 with □﹦, suggesting the lost graph was 屯 or 惷. Here 閔閔 and 亓民屯屯 follow MS B. 竊竊 follows MS A: the 1980 edition read 察, but Chen Jian showed that the double curved strokes under 宀 do not fit 祭, and re-read the graph as 竊, a loan for 察 "prying"; MS B writes 察. The last clause, 亓邦夬夬, follows MS A, 夬夬 being read as 缺缺 "split, deficient"; in MS B everything after 亓 is lost, and the Jicheng changes the old restoration 民缺缺 to begin with 國, after the Peking University manuscript. Wang Bi reads 其政悶悶，其民淳淳；其政察察，其民缺缺. (Jicheng IV, p. 29, n. 116; pp. 30–31, n. 117; p. 202, n. 93)
  • 𢢸福之所倚 / 福禍之所伏: Follows MS A. MS A writes the first 禍 in the variant form 𢢸; the 1980 edition wrote the second 禍 in the same variant, but the Jicheng notes that the original has the ordinary 禍. In MS B the small fragment bearing 福 had been mounted too high, leaving a gap before 之所伏, and the 1980 edition read 福，【禍】之所伏; the Jicheng, guided by the offset, lowers the fragment and, seeing remains of 禍 on it, reads 禍，福之所伏, suspecting that the scribe wrote 禍福 and then skipped to 之所伏, omitting the five graphs 之所倚福禍. Wang Bi has 兮 in both lines. (Jicheng IV, p. 31, n. 118; p. 202, n. 94)
  • 孰知亓極: Follows MS B; of MS A only 孰 is read directly by the Jicheng, the rest falling in two lines that are entirely lost. (Jicheng IV, p. 29, n. 116)
  • 亓无正也: Follows MS B; its 亓, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is now read from the offset on the backing silk. Wang Bi reads 其無正, without 也. (Jicheng IV, p. 202, n. 95)
  • 正復為奇: Follows MS B, whose 復為奇, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is now read directly from the offset. (Jicheng IV, p. 202, n. 96)
  • 善復為: Follows MS B. The 1980 edition restored 妖 after 為; the Jicheng finds from the offset that there is no room for it, so it is an omission by the scribe. Wang Bi reads 善復為妖. (Jicheng IV, p. 202, n. 97)
  • 人之𢘻也: Follows MS B; 𢘻 is read as 迷 "to be lost", and 人, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is now read from the offset. Wang Bi reads 人之迷, without 也. (Jicheng IV, p. 202, n. 98)
  • 亓日固久矣: Follows MS B; Wang Bi lacks 矣.
  • 是以方而不割: Follows MS B; Wang Bi reads 是以聖人方而不割, whereas the silk lacks 聖人.
  • 兼而不刺直而不紲: Follows MS B. 刺 is the 1980 reading; the Jicheng, following Chen Jian, judges it incompatible with the form and re-reads the graph as 利, a loan for 劌 "to gash", Chen Jian adding that it is more likely a graph with 刀 and phonetic 末, also read as 劌. Wang Bi reads 廉而不劌，直而不肆. (Jicheng IV, p. 202, n. 99)
  • 光而不眺: Follows MS B; Wang Bi reads 光而不燿.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The crux of this chapter: the weighing of lenient against harsh in governing, and the truth of calamity and fortune leaning on each other — closing at the sage's De of squareness. Here silk text and Wang Bi differ in ways that must be watched.

閔閔 and Wang Bi's 悶悶 are both the look of mild simplicity; 屯屯 and 淳淳 are both the look of thick honesty. Water's nature is soft, so it moistens the ten thousand things without contending; governing is the same: lenient, and the people grow honest; harsh, and the people grow chipped. The 察察 government is the government of steep law and dense decree, of prying exactness. Governing by non-doing patterns itself on water, which benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still, ruling by the people's own self-so — the point of "water benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still."

As for 兼而不刺, that is the silk text; Wang Bi reads 廉而不劌, "edged but not gashing." 兼 is embracing — holding much together; 廉 is the honed corner — angular integrity. 兼 leans toward the Daoist's harmony, 廉 toward the Confucian's rectitude. Embracing-but-not-piercing names the sage's capacity — wide without invading. Square-but-not-cutting — upright without wounding. Shining-but-not-dazzling — the sage's De held within, never flashed to blind. Straight-but-not-binding (紲; Wang Bi reads 肆) — walking the straight way without forcing it on things.

"It has no fixed straightness: the straight turns strange again, the good turns uncanny again" (manuscript A is wholly lost here; this is manuscript B, which lacks the final 訞, evidently dropped by the copyist and read in from the pattern of the text) — the sense of "not contending, hence without fault." Men's faults come of contending; to be faultless, first drop the contending heart. This is not relativism; it is the watching of times and turns: straight and good are not fixed essences but positions that move with the hour. So with calamity and fortune: no calamity is absolute, no fortune absolute; each leans on the other, each crouches inside the other, a ring without an end. This is the plain grain of things, not fatalist gloom.

"Men have been lost in this a long day indeed" (manuscript B writes 𢘻 for 迷; manuscript A is lost): lost in gripping calamity-and-fortune, straight-and-strange, and long lost. Laozi does not scold; he sighs. The long-lost are hard to wake, so the sage does not force men awake: he sets himself square without cutting, embracing without piercing, straight without binding, shining without dazzling. All four keep their substance and sheathe their use. The square has edges and wounds no one with them; the embracing can hold and coerces no one with it; the straight is upright and bullies no one with it; the shining is bright and blinds no one with it.

Examined against the making of the Book of De — collating the good words of highest De from high antiquity through middle and late, sorted kind by kind — the silk text lacks the two graphs 聖人 here, saying directly, "therefore: square, but cutting no one." The sense runs deeper for it: this De of squareness is not the sage's private property but the Dao's own grain. Doing without contending is the completion of De entire. When the uncontending heart reaches through high and low, the government of shining-without-dazzling stands complete.
