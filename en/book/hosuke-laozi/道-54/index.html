The Book of Dao · Chapter Ten · Non-doing, Nothing Undone

English Translation

Bearing soul and body, holding the One (抱一) — can you keep them from parting? Gathering the qi (气) to utmost softness (摶氣至柔) — can you be as a newborn?

Polishing the dark mirror (脩除玄鑒) — can you leave it without a fleck? Loving the people, keeping the state — can you do it without using knowing (毋以知)?

The gates of heaven opening and closing — can you play the female? Seeing clear to the four reaches — can you do it without using knowing?

To give them birth, to rear them; to give birth and not possess; to raise them and not rule them: this is called dark De (玄德).

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

Six questions in this chapter — the complete work of cultivating the self. Bearing soul and body, holding the One: soul and frame one with the Dao — the root of the whole practice. Heshang Gong reads 戴 as 載, "to bear" — one sense. Holding the One is not gripping with force; it is the heart gathered into the Dao so the spirit does not scatter.

Gathering the qi to utmost softness: Wang Bi writes 專氣致柔. 摶 and 專 are near — both mean keeping the essential qi gathered, never letting it gallop off. Gathering the qi down to softness is returning to the newborn's first state: whole, unscattered, soft without brittleness — the direction of everyone who cultivates the Dao.

Polishing the dark mirror: the Wang Bi recension writes 滌除玄覽, "washing the dark seeing clean." 鑒 is the old mirror — the basin of still water; 覽 is contemplative seeing. Both name the heart as a dark mirror, luminous by nature. What gathers on it is not the mirror going blind — it is the dust of appetite. 脩除, polishing and removing, is emptying the heart and keeping the straight, clearing away the screen of cleverness. This pairs with the Great Learning's rectifying of the heart: when the heart is not present, one looks and does not see. The dark seeing is bright by root — the trouble is only the film not yet cleared away.

Loving the people, keeping the state: Wang Bi writes 愛國治民. 栝 borrows for 治, "to govern" — the silk text's graph is the older. Preside over all-under-heaven by the Dao, not troubling it with cleverness: loving the people without leaning on cleverness.

The gates of heaven opening and closing: heaven's gates are the door of creation, the hinge where yin and yang take turns. Playing the female is receiving without refusing, still without going first — answering the ten thousand changes with empty absence.

"Seeing clear to the four reaches — can you do it without knowing?" Wang Bi here has 能無為乎, "can you do it by non-doing" — a small divergence of sense. 毋以知 is knowing without putting knowing to work: not the witlessness of dead wood, but true knowing without a grip.

The close: "to give them birth, to rear them; to give birth and not possess; to grow and not master" — Wang Bi adds one clause, "to work and not lean on it." The silk text's 弗有 and 弗宰 are steeper than 不有 and 不宰: the Dao's dark work lies in not gripping what it has completed. The six questions all come home here: cultivate the self to join the Dao, and end in dark De — and the crux of dark De is not how high the attainment, but mastering nothing, owning nothing, gripping nothing.
