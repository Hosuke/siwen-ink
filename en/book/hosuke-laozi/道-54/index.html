The Book of Dao · Chapter Ten · Non-doing, Nothing Undone

English Translation

Bearing soul and body, holding the One (抱一) — can you keep them from parting? Gathering the qi (氣) to utmost softness (槫氣至柔, 槫 read as 摶) — can you be as a newborn?

Polishing the dark mirror (脩除玄監, 監 read as 鑒) — can you leave it without a fleck? Loving the people, keeping the state — can you do it without using knowing (毋以知)?

The gates of heaven opening and closing — can you play the female? Seeing clear to the four reaches — can you do it without using knowing?

To give them birth, to rear them; to give birth and not possess; to raise them and not rule them: this is called dark De (玄德).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Manuscript A (MS A) is badly damaged in this chapter: only 能毋疵乎 and 生之畜之 survive intact, and most lines are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 戴營袙抱一 / 能毋离乎: Both lines are lost in MS A and follow MS B. Gao Ming's transcription gives MS B's first graph as 載; the Jicheng prints 戴, read as 載 "to bear". 离 is read as 離 "to part". Wang Bi has 載營魄抱一，能無離乎.
  • 槫氣至柔能嬰兒乎: Follows MS B, with 槫 read as 摶 "to gather" and 至 as 致 "to bring to". In MS A the two damaged graphs after 氣 were restored as 至柔 in the 1980 edition; the Jicheng, finding the remaining strokes insufficient to identify them, marks them as illegible. Wang Bi has 專氣致柔. (Jicheng IV, p. 46, n. 37)
  • 脩除玄監: Follows MS B; MS A reads 脩除玄藍. The Jicheng reads both 監 and 藍 as 鑒 "mirror". Wang Bi has 滌除玄覽.
  • 能毋疵乎: Follows MS A; MS B reads 能毋有疵乎, with an extra 有. Wang Bi has 能無疵乎.
  • 愛民栝國: MS A preserves only 愛, which the Jicheng reads directly; the rest follows MS B, whose 栝 is read as 活 "to keep alive". The 1980 edition restored MS A's lacuna as 愛民活國; the Jicheng changes 國 to 邦, arguing that MS A, which does not avoid the taboo on 邦, would certainly have used 邦 in this context. Wang Bi has 愛國治民. (Jicheng IV, p. 46, n. 39)
  • 能毋以知乎 / 天門啟闔 / 能為雌乎 / 明白四達: All lost in MS A and supplied from MS B. Wang Bi has 開 for 啟, and 能無知乎 for the first line.
  • 能毋以知乎 (second occurrence): Follows MS B, which repeats 毋以知 "without using knowing". Wang Bi has 能無為乎. The 1980 edition restored MS A's lacuna after Fu Yi's text as 能毋以為乎; the Jicheng corrects this, holding that MS A must have read 毋以知. (Jicheng IV, p. 46, n. 40)
  • 生之 / 畜之: The manuscripts agree.
  • 生而弗有: 有 is lost in MS A and supplied from MS B. Wang Bi follows this with 為而不恃 "works and does not lean on it", which MS B and the Peking University manuscript lack; MS A is damaged here, but the Jicheng infers from the number of missing graphs that it too lacked the line. (Jicheng IV, p. 208, n. 13)
  • 長而弗宰也 / 是胃玄德: MS A preserves only the final 德; the lines follow MS B. Both manuscripts have 弗; Wang Bi reads 長而不宰，是謂玄德, without 也.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

Six questions in this chapter — the complete work of cultivating the self. Bearing soul and body (戴營袙, 袙 read as 魄), holding the One: soul and frame one with the Dao — the root of the whole practice. Heshang Gong reads 戴 as 載, "to bear" — one sense. Holding the One is not gripping with force; it is the heart gathered into the Dao so the spirit does not scatter.

Gathering the qi to utmost softness (槫氣至柔, 槫 read as 摶): Wang Bi writes 專氣致柔. 摶 and 專 are near — both mean keeping the essential qi gathered, never letting it gallop off. Gathering the qi down to softness is returning to the newborn's first state: whole, unscattered, soft without brittleness — the direction of everyone who cultivates the Dao.

Polishing the dark mirror (manuscript B, 脩除玄監; manuscript A writes 藍, and the Jicheng reads both as 鑒): the Wang Bi recension writes 滌除玄覽, "washing the dark seeing clean." 鑒 is the old mirror — the basin of still water; 覽 is contemplative seeing. Both name the heart as a dark mirror, luminous by nature. What gathers on it is not the mirror going blind — it is the dust of appetite. 脩除, polishing and removing, is emptying the heart and keeping the straight, clearing away the screen of cleverness. This pairs with the Great Learning's rectifying of the heart: when the heart is not present, one looks and does not see. The dark seeing is bright by root — the trouble is only the film not yet cleared away.

Loving the people, keeping the state (愛民栝國): Wang Bi writes 愛國治民. 栝 the Jicheng reads as 活, "to keep alive" (manuscript A is damaged, and the Jicheng restores it as 愛民活邦) — to love the people and so keep their state alive, near in sense to Wang Bi's 治民, "governing the people," but plainer in speech. The silk text is the older. Preside over all-under-heaven by the Dao, not troubling it with cleverness: loving the people without leaning on cleverness.

The gates of heaven opening and closing: heaven's gates are the door of creation, the hinge where yin and yang take turns. Playing the female is receiving without refusing, still without going first — answering the ten thousand changes with empty absence.

"Seeing clear to the four reaches — can you do it without using knowing?" Wang Bi here has 能無為乎, "can you do it by non-doing" — a small divergence of sense. 毋以知 is knowing without putting knowing to work: not the witlessness of dead wood, but true knowing without a grip.

The close: "to give them birth, to rear them; to give birth and not possess; to raise them and not rule them" — Wang Bi adds one clause, "to work and not lean on it." The silk text's 弗有 and 弗宰 are steeper than 不有 and 不宰: the Dao's dark work lies in not gripping what it has completed. The six questions all come home here: cultivate the self to join the Dao, and end in dark De — and the crux of dark De is not how high the attainment, but ruling nothing, owning nothing, gripping nothing.
