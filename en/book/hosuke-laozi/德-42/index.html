The Book of De · Chapter Forty-two · The Way of Heaven

English Translation

The Way of Heaven is like the drawing of a bow: what is high it presses down; what is low it raises up; what has surplus it takes from; what falls short it makes good (補).

Thus the Way of Heaven takes from the surplus to give (益) to the wanting; the way of man takes from the wanting to offer to the surplus.

Who can have surplus, and take it to offer up to Heaven? Only one who has the Dao.

Hence the sage works and does not possess; completes the work and does not dwell in it — he has no wish to be seen as worthy.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Manuscript A (MS A) is damaged at both ends of this chapter: the first two lines, 人之道, 云不足而奉又余 and everything from 唯又道者乎 on follow Manuscript B (MS B); □云有余而益不足 is damaged in both and follows the graphs MS B preserves; the rest follows MS A. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 天之道: Follows MS B. MS A reads 天下□□; the original editors noted that no other text has 下 here and that it is an interpolated graph, and the Jicheng marks it as such: 天{下}【之道】. (Jicheng IV, p. 38, n. 195)
  • 酉張弓也: Follows MS B; 酉 is read as 猶 "like". MS A reads □□□者也, with 者 before 也. Wang Bi reads 其猶張弓與.
  • 高者印之 / 下者舉之: Follows MS A; both manuscripts agree. 印 is read as 抑 "to press down", which is what Wang Bi writes.
  • 有餘者𢿃之: Follows MS A. MS A's 𢿃 (員 with 攵), read as 損 "to take from", occurs three times in this chapter (it has sometimes been transcribed as 敗, which drops the 口 and gives a different graph). MS B reads 有余者云之, with 余 read as 餘 and 云 also read as 損. Wang Bi reads 有餘者損之.
  • 不足者補之: Follows MS A; in MS B 補 is lost.
  • 故天之道: Follows MS A; in MS B all four graphs are lost. Wang Bi lacks 故.
  • □云有余而益不足: Damaged in both manuscripts; it follows MS B, which preserves more, though its first graph is lost. MS A reads 𢿃有□□□□□. Before 不足 the 1980 edition restored 補 after the received texts; the Jicheng replaces it with □, since MS B has 益 here and the Peking University manuscript 奉, and what MS A wrote cannot be determined. Wang Bi reads 損有餘而補不足. (Jicheng IV, p. 38, n. 196)
  • 人之道: Follows MS B; lost in MS A. Wang Bi reads 人之道則不然.
  • 云不足而奉又余: Follows MS B; 云 is read as 損, and 又余 as 有餘. MS A reads □不然𢿃□□□奉有餘, with 不然 "not so", as in Wang Bi; its 然 is written in a corrupt form. Wang Bi reads 損不足以奉有餘.
  • 孰能有餘而有以取奉於天者: Follows MS A, which also has a final 乎. MS B reads 夫孰能又余而□□奉於天者, opening with 夫. The two lost graphs after 而 were restored in 1980 as 有以; Gao Ming judged that this does not fit the sense, and the Jicheng, following the Peking University manuscript, restores 有（又）取 instead. Wang Bi reads 孰能有餘以奉天下. (Jicheng IV, p. 38, n. 197; p. 204, n. 145)
  • 唯又道者乎: Follows MS B; 又 is read as 有. In MS A this line is lost. The 1980 edition restored it from MS B as 唯有道者乎; the Jicheng, following the Peking University manuscript, restores 唯有道者也. Wang Bi reads 唯有道者. (Jicheng IV, p. 38, n. 197)
  • 是以𦔻人為而弗又 / 成功而弗居也若此: Follows MS B; lost in MS A. Wang Bi reads 是以聖人為而不恃，功成而不處, without 若此 "like this".
  • 亓不欲見賢也: Follows MS B; MS A preserves only 見賢也. Wang Bi reads 其不欲見賢.

Commentary

The Way of Heaven is like the drawing of a bow. The bow's way is to set crooked and straight aright; the Dao's way in all-under-heaven is to even high and low. Watch the bow drawn: the string a foot too high is pressed down; the string an inch too low is raised; the surplus is taken from; the wanting is made good — the image of the Way of Heaven. The Changes says of the hexagram Qian ䷎ (Modesty), "the noble man takes from the much to give to the little," and of Yi ䷩ (Increase), wind and thunder aiding each other. The sage watches the images and takes his law from them — the crux of ordering the world lies here.

The Way of Heaven takes from the surplus to give to the wanting: this is the sense of evening out, not merely of patching. The Shuowen: "益 is abundance"; "補 is to make a garment whole." 益 adds until there is bounty and breadth; 補 fills the gap to just-enough. Heaven's heart is broad and bountiful like this, so the ten thousand things each rest in their allotments and nothing withers at the edge. The way of man is otherwise: taking from the wanting to offer to the surplus — tilting toward the lopsided, ever farther from Heaven.

Collating the silk text against Wang Bi, three points. First, manuscript A opens 天下□□, where manuscript B, like Wang Bi, has 天之道. The first editors note that no other text has 下 and judge it an intrusion, and the Jicheng agrees; manuscript A, then, also reads "the Way of Heaven," set against "thus the Way of Heaven" and "the way of man" below, and the line between Heaven and man comes clear. Second, manuscript B has 益不足 (manuscript A's graph is lost; the Peking University manuscript has 奉), "giving to the wanting," for Wang Bi's 補不足, "patching the wanting" — 益 and 補 differ in depth. Third, the silk text has 取奉於天者, "taking to offer up to Heaven," for Wang Bi's 奉天下, "offering to all-under-heaven": the sage gathers in his surplus and returns it to Heaven, never daring to hold it privately.

The sage works and does not possess; completes the work and does not dwell in it; has no wish to be seen as worthy — three clauses, one thread, all of the highest De's non-doing. Gain and loss do not move the heart: the work done, the body withdraws, and the De does not cease; self-boasting and self-flaunting have no place in the man of the Dao. The bow hits because it does not lean on its full draw; the Dao lasts because it does not dwell in its merit. The sage knows "presence gives the advantage, absence gives the use" — so he steps back and the work endures, sets himself aside and his De spreads wide. The world knows only that taking is profit; it does not know that letting go is use — far from the sage indeed.
