The Book of Dao · Chapter Fifteen · Never Full

English Translation

Of old, those who […] worked the Dao were minute and subtle, darkly far-reaching (微眇玄達) — deep beyond marking. Just because they were beyond marking, I strain to sketch their look:

Cautious, ah (與呵) — as if crossing a winter stream. Hesitant, ah — as if fearing the neighbors on four sides. Solemn, ah — like a guest. Yielding, ah — like ice about to melt. Unshaped, ah — like unhewn wood. Murky, ah — like turbid water. Dignified, ah (𣼥呵) — like one bathing clean (浴; the Jicheng reads it as 谷, "like a valley").

Turbid — yet stilled, it slowly clears. At rest — yet once stirred, it slowly comes alive.

He who keeps this Dao does not desire fullness. Hence he can stay worn (𧝬, i.e. 敝) — and never become complete.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Manuscript A (MS A) is badly damaged in this chapter: only the three lines from 深不可志 to 故強為之容 follow MS A, and the rest is supplied from Manuscript B (MS B); the first line is damaged in both. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 古之□為道者: Damaged in both manuscripts. MS A has lost the whole line. In MS B the graph above 為 is damaged; the 1980 editors saw 人 over 山, i.e. 仙, as a loan for 善 "good at", but the Jicheng finds this doubtful and leaves the graph unread. Wang Bi has 古之善為士者, with 士 "officer" for the silk 道. (Jicheng IV, p. 209, n. 22)
  • 微眇玄達: Lost in MS A and supplied from MS B, whose 微 is written in a variant form; 眇 is read as 妙. Wang Bi has 微妙玄通.
  • 深不可志 / 夫唯不可志 / 故強為之容: Follow MS A; MS B agrees. 志 is read as 識 "to know, to mark".
  • 曰與呵亓若冬涉水猷呵亓若畏四𡜓: Follows MS B; MS A reads 曰與呵其若冬□□□□□□畏四□. MS B's 猷 is read as 猶; for 𡜓 the Jicheng prints a form with 厸 over 文, which has no code point, read as 鄰 "neighbor"; Gao Ming's photograph shows 吅 over 女. MS A writes 其 and MS B 亓 throughout the chapter. The silk text has 曰 "it is said", and its 呵 corresponds to Wang Bi's 兮; Wang Bi reads 豫焉若冬涉川，猶兮若畏四鄰.
  • 嚴呵亓若客: Follows MS B; in MS A 嚴 is lost, while 呵 is read directly by the Jicheng. Wang Bi has 儼兮其若容, with 容 "appearance" for 客 "guest".
  • 渙呵亓若淩澤: Follows MS B; MS A reads 渙呵其若淩澤, its 若 absent from Gao Ming's transcription but present in the Jicheng. 淩澤 is read as 凌釋 "ice melting". Wang Bi has 渙兮若冰之將釋.
  • 沌呵亓若樸: Follows MS B. MS A reads □呵其若楃: the left side of the first graph is 王, the right lies at the broken edge of the fragment; Gao Ming gives 王 beside 口, the 1980 edition a lacuna, and the Jicheng prints 王 plus an illegible element, suggesting that the graph was originally 王 with 屯. MS A's 楃 is read by the Jicheng as 樸 "uncarved wood". Wang Bi has 敦兮其若樸. (Jicheng IV, p. 49, n. 60)
  • 湷呵亓若濁: Follows MS B; MS A preserves only 湷. Wang Bi has 混兮其若濁 and places it after 曠兮其若谷, whereas in the silk text the "turbid" line precedes the 浴 line.
  • 𣼥呵亓若浴: Follows MS B, whose first graph is 莊 with the water radical, clear on the black-and-white plate; the Jicheng reads 浴 as 谷 "valley". In MS A the first graph is damaged; the 1980 editors restored it after MS B as 壯 with the water radical, but the Jicheng withdraws this, since the received texts have 曠 and the Peking University manuscript 廣, and MS A need not have agreed with MS B. Wang Bi has 曠兮其若谷. (Jicheng IV, p. 49, n. 62)
  • 濁而靜之徐清: Follows MS B; MS A reads 濁而情之余清, its 情 and 余 read by the Jicheng as 靜 "to still" and 徐 "slowly". MS B's 靜 is printed in the Jicheng with 爭 on the left and 青 on the right, a form without a code point, and is given here as 靜. Wang Bi has the rhetorical question 孰能濁以靜之徐清.
  • 女以重之徐生: Follows MS B; MS A writes 余 for 徐. The Jicheng treats 女 as a miswriting of 安 "at rest" and reads 重 as 動 "to stir". Wang Bi has 孰能安以久動之徐生, with an extra 久.
  • 葆此道□□欲盈: Follows MS B, in which the two graphs after 道 are lost; the Jicheng restores 者不. MS A reads 葆此道不欲盈, without 者; its 葆 is printed in the Jicheng with the 彳 radical, a form without a code point, and treated as a miswriting of 葆. Because the base text follows MS B here, 不 appears as a lacuna. Wang Bi has 保此道者不欲盈.
  • 是以能𧝬而不成: Follows MS B. MS B lacks the preceding line 夫唯不欲盈, which MS A has and which the Jicheng supplies in MS B as a scribal omission; in MS A 以能 is read directly by the Jicheng, while 𧝬而不 is lost. MS B's 𧝬, 敝 over 衣, is read as 敝 "worn". Wang Bi has 夫唯不盈，故能蔽不新成. (Jicheng IV, p. 209, n. 24)

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The Dao cannot be gotten by form or name, cannot be spied out by cleverness. Those of old who were good at working the Dao (manuscript B has 古之□為道者; the original editors' note suspects the lost graph is 善, "good at," which the Jicheng leaves open; Wang Bi has 古之善為士者) had a De minute and subtle, darkly far-reaching — deep, far, beyond marking. The silk text's 微眇玄達 against Wang Bi's 微妙玄通: one graph apart, the depths differ. 達 is going through and arriving — straighter than 通, "passing": there is nowhere the Dao does not reach, and reaching is the Dao's own end.

Being beyond marking, I strain to sketch their look. Cautious, as if crossing a winter stream; hesitant, as if fearing the neighbors on four sides; solemn, like a guest; yielding, like ice about to melt; unshaped, like unhewn wood; murky, like turbid water; dignified, like one bathing clean (𣼥 and 浴, which the Jicheng reads as 曠 and 谷, are taken here as written). Seven faces, all showing a De that bends low and gathers inward, never showing itself — the point being that the man of the Dao takes up residence in no single shape.

The chapter's two great variants come down to these. First, 濁而情之余清 (manuscript A; manuscript B has 濁而靜之徐清, and the Jicheng reads 情 as 靜 and 余 as 徐) against Wang Bi's 孰能濁以靜之徐清. Wang Bi's is a question — roundabout in wording, deep in meaning; the silk text states the effect flatly — plain in wording, true in meaning. Read as written, 情 is the self-so of things; 靜 is the work of stilling. Turbid, yet able to be stilled: by its nature it slowly clears — the effect of light born inside the empty. The Doctrine of the Mean: "reach centered harmony, and heaven and earth take their places, the ten thousand things are nourished" — the same law.

Second, manuscript B's 能𧝬而不成 against Wang Bi's 蔽不新成 — one graph apart, and the senses are a sky apart. 𧝬, 敝 written over 衣, is worn, frayed, the self-so of decline; 不成 is never completing. "Diminish and diminish again, down to non-doing": diminished to where nothing is left to diminish — that is 敝 at its utmost, and it is no word of decline but the real ground of the highest De's non-doing. Only because he does not desire fullness can he "reach utmost emptiness, keep the natures and their traces" (守情表 read as written; the Jicheng reads it as 守靜篤, "keep stillness entire") — can stay worn and never complete. As for those hustling after the new among names and ranks: they do not know that fullness cannot last and the new cannot be leaned on. The world gallops and does not know where to stop — and in the end the full invite loss, and the new wears out fast. Only those of old, good at working the Dao, hold unhewn wood and keep the chaos whole, worn to the end of their days and never complete — this is the gate of the Dao.
