The Book of De · Chapter Thirteen · Life and Death

English Translation

Out into life, in into death.

The companions of life are three in ten; the companions of death are three in ten; and the people who live for living — whose every move goes toward the ground of death — are also three in ten.

Why is that so? Because they live for living.

It is said: one good at holding to life walks the hills without shunning rhinoceros or tiger, enters an army without donning armor and blade. The rhinoceros finds nowhere to thrust its horn; the tiger finds nowhere to set its claws; the weapon finds nowhere to sink […] edge.

Why is that so? Because there is no ground of death in him.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

This chapter follows Manuscript A (MS A), except that the first three lines, the two lines 蓋聞善執生者 and 陵行不辟𦊽虎, and the second 夫何故也 are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B), and 兵无所容□刃 is damaged in both. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 出生入死: MS A has a chapter-division dot above 出. The 1980 edition bracketed 出 as a restoration; Chen Jian observed that the left side of the overlaid 出 is still visible, and the Jicheng reads it directly and adds the dot. 入死 is lost in MS A and supplied from MS B; in MS B too, 出 is now read directly from the mirror-image offset. (Jicheng IV, p. 19, n. 61; p. 200, n. 48)
  • 生之徒十又三 / 死之徒十又三: Follows MS B, which writes 又; MS A preserves only 有 in the first line and reads 十有三 in the second. MS B's 徒十又三 and the 死 of the second line were bracketed as restorations in the 1980 edition; the Jicheng reads them from the offset on the backing silk. Wang Bi has 十有三. (Jicheng IV, p. 200, nn. 49, 50)
  • 而民生生勭皆之死地之十有三: Follows MS A. Both manuscripts write 生 with a repetition mark, read 生生 "living for living"; MS A writes 勭, MS B 僮, both read as 動 "to move". Wang Bi reads 人之生，動之死地，亦十有三, without 民 and 皆, and with 亦十有三 for 之十有三.
  • 夫何故也: Both manuscripts agree. MS B's 夫 was bracketed as a restoration in the 1980 edition; the Jicheng reads it directly from a fragment stuck to the backing silk. (Jicheng IV, p. 200, n. 51)
  • 以亓生生也: Follows MS A; MS B lacks 也. Wang Bi reads 以其生生之厚, adding 之厚 "thickly".
  • 蓋聞善執生者: In MS A 聞善 is lost and supplied from MS B. Wang Bi has 攝 for 執.
  • 陵行不辟𦊽虎: Follows MS B. In MS A 辟 is lost and the rhinoceros is written 矢; MS B writes 𦊽 (罒 over 豕); both are read as 兕. Received texts all have 陸 "land" for 陵 "hills", and most have 遇 "meet" for 辟 "shun": Wang Bi reads 陸行不遇兕虎. (Jicheng IV, p. 20, n. 65)
  • 入軍不被甲兵: Follows MS A; MS B and the Peking University manuscript read 兵革, MS B writing 革 at the head of the next line. Received texts agree with MS A. (Jicheng IV, p. 20, n. 66)
  • 矢无所椯亓角: Follows MS A; of this line MS B keeps only 𦊽无. 椯 is shared by the Peking University manuscript, while received texts mostly have 投 "to thrust". The original editors glossed 椯 as 揣, which yields poor sense; the Jicheng deletes that gloss and, following Luo Zhenyu and Chen Jian, reads it as 拄 "to butt, to prop against". Wang Bi reads 兕無所投其角. (Jicheng IV, p. 20, n. 67)
  • 虎无所昔亓蚤: Follows MS A; MS B keeps only 亓蚤. 昔 is read as 措 "to set", 蚤 as 爪 "claw"; Wang Bi reads 虎無所措其爪.
  • 兵无所容□刃: Damaged in both. MS A keeps 兵无所容, MS B keeps 兵 and 刃; 刃 was bracketed as a restoration in the 1980 edition and is now read directly from the offset. The missing graph is 其 in Wang Bi. (Jicheng IV, p. 200, n. 52)
  • 夫何故也 (second): 夫 is lost in MS A, so the line follows MS B, whose 夫何故 was bracketed as a restoration in the 1980 edition and is now read directly from the offset. (Jicheng IV, p. 200, n. 53)
  • 以亓无死地焉: Follows MS A; MS B reads 以亓无死地焉也. Its 死地焉 was bracketed as a restoration in the 1980 edition, and 也 was not restored at all; the Jicheng reads all four graphs from the offset on the backing silk. MS B then has a further 故, which the Jicheng marks as superfluous. Wang Bi reads 以其無死地. (Jicheng IV, p. 20, n. 68; p. 200, nn. 54, 55)

Commentary

Yaomiao says: life and death are the gathering and scattering of one qi (氣), not a gain and a loss but the ordinary course of transformation. In this chapter Laozi watches the world come out into life and go in into death, and looks for the way through; the intent runs deep. Of the people of all-under-heaven, the companions of life are three in ten; the companions of death, three in ten; and those whose living-for-living moves them all toward the ground of death, three in ten again: all three arise from the count of what is so of itself, and together they make nine. Nine parts in ten walk the road to death; what remains is the one man good at holding to life. Hence "it is said" (蓋聞), marking how rarely it is heard.

The silk text writes 而民生生 where Wang Bi has 人之生. The turning particle 而 makes the sentence bite: precisely those who live for living are the ones whose every motion enters the ground of death. That is the root sense of "out into life, in into death." And 勭皆之死地 (勭 is 動), "every move goes to the ground of death," is steeper than Wang Bi's wording. Again: the silk text has 陵行不辟𦊽虎, "walks the hills without shunning rhinoceros and tiger," where Wang Bi has 陸行不遇, "travels the land without meeting them." 陵 is hill-country; 辟 is to shun. One graph, and active parts from passive: the silk text's man walks near the beasts and does not shun them; because he has no ground of death in him, the tiger cannot touch him; Wang Bi's "does not meet" is a passive escape, mere luck. The one good at holding to life is actively without ground of death — he is not passively spared by fortune. The silk text's sense is the stronger; follow it.

As for 以亓生生也 against Wang Bi's 以其生生之厚: the silk text lacks 之厚, "thickly." To live-for-living thickly is appetite without limit, savor without satiety. The Guanyinzi speaks of life and death as the transformations of qi; life and death are qi gathering and scattering: the same point. The man of fullest De is like a naked babe (bones soft, sinews weak, grip firm) because he is without desire. He who thickens his life wounds it: greedy for savor, he is cut down by appetite; over-tending the body, he drowns in the tending. To have no ground of death is not to be diamond-bodied; it is a heart without grasping, a life not thickened. Alas, you who would seize life and thicken it — that is ladling boiling water to stop the boil. Be plain as water instead: thicken nothing, and the length of life is yours.
