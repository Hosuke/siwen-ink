The Book of De · Chapter Seventeen · Skilled in Beholding

English Translation

What is well planted is not pulled up; what is well held does not slip away: sons and grandsons keep the sacrifice unbroken.

Cultivate it in the body, and its De (德) is genuine. Cultivate it in the house, and its De has surplus. Cultivate it in the village, and its De grows long. Cultivate it in the state, and its De grows ample. Cultivate it in all-under-heaven, and its De grows broad.

By the body, behold bodies; by the house, behold houses; by the village, behold villages; by the state, behold states; by all-under-heaven, behold all-under-heaven.

How do I know that all-under-heaven is so? By this.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Manuscript A (MS A) is heavily damaged in this chapter: only 以家觀家, 以鄉觀鄉 and 以邦觀邦 follow MS A, the rest follows Manuscript B (MS B), and 善抱者不□ and 吾何□知天下之然茲 are damaged in both. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 善建者不拔: Follows MS B. In MS A 者不 is lost; the 1980 edition restored it from MS B and the received texts, and the Jicheng follows. MS B's 不拔, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is now read directly from the offset on the backing silk. (Jicheng IV, p. 24, n. 88; p. 201, n. 73)
  • 善抱者不□: Damaged in both. MS A is wholly lost. MS B's 善抱者不, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is now read directly from the offset; of the last graph the offset shows 兌 on the right with the left side unclear, so the Jicheng replaces the 1980 restoration 脫 with □. Wang Bi reads 善抱者不脫. (Jicheng IV, p. 201, n. 74)
  • 子孫以祭祀不絕: Follows MS B; in MS A 不絕 is lost. MS A writes 祭 with 肰 at the top, a corrupt form easily confused with 然; the 1980 edition normalized it to 祭, while the Jicheng records the corrupt form. Wang Bi has 輟 for 絕. (Jicheng IV, p. 24, n. 89)
  • 脩之身 / 亓德乃真: Lost in MS A; follows MS B. The same pattern holds for 脩之家, 脩之鄉, 脩之國 and 脩之天下; Wang Bi adds 於 after 之 in each.
  • 亓德有餘: Follows MS B; MS A keeps only 餘. Wang Bi reads 其德乃餘.
  • 脩之國 / 亓德乃夆: Follows MS B; MS A is lost. 夆 is read as 豐 "ample", which rhymes with 邦; MS B avoids the name of the Han founder, Liu Bang, by writing 國 for 邦, and the rhyme is lost. The "Jie Lao" chapter of the Han Feizi quotes 修之邦，其德乃豐, as do the Fu Yi and Fan Yingyuan texts. Chen Jian notes that the lower part of 夆 is written 廾, and the Jicheng transcribes it accordingly. (Jicheng IV, p. 201, n. 75)
  • 亓德乃博: Follows MS B. MS A's 亓德 was bracketed as a restoration in 1980; the Jicheng reads it directly from a small fragment newly joined at the end of the line, the two graphs after it being lost. MS B's 博 was transcribed 愽 in 1980 and glossed 溥, while Gao Ming read it simply as 博; Chen Jian shows that its right side is actually 蒪, and the Jicheng transcribes it strictly, reading it as 溥 "wide". Wang Bi reads 其德乃普. (Jicheng IV, p. 24, n. 91; p. 201, n. 76)
  • 以身觀身: Follows MS B; in MS A 觀 is lost. Wang Bi begins the line with 故.
  • 以鄉觀鄉 / 以邦觀邦: Follows MS A. The lacuna before MS B's 以國觀國 does not hold as many graphs as MS A, so MS B probably lacked the line 以鄉觀鄉, as does the "Jie Lao" chapter of the Han Feizi; the received texts have it. MS B writes 國 for 邦, as does Wang Bi. (Jicheng IV, p. 201, n. 77)
  • 吾何□知天下之然茲: Damaged in both. In MS A everything after 天下 is lost, but its 𢦏 is read directly from a newly joined fragment; in MS B 吾何 and 知 are read from the offset, and 以 is lost. Both 茲 and 𢦏 are read as 哉. Wang Bi reads 吾何以知天下然哉, without 之. (Jicheng IV, p. 24, n. 91)
  • 以此: Follows MS B, whose 此, bracketed as a restoration in 1980, is now read directly from the offset. MS A's 以此, also bracketed in 1980, is now read directly from the newly joined fragment, which shows one further damaged graph after it, unidentifiable but belonging to this chapter. (Jicheng IV, p. 24, n. 91; p. 201, n. 78)

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

"What is well planted is not pulled up; what is well held does not slip away": planting means rooting within, not building without; holding means keeping the One in the heart, not clutching a thing in the hand. Rooted within, no outer force can pull it up; keeping the One with no-heart, it never slips. The two lines say one thing: De stands inside, not outside. When De fills the body, its bounty flows of itself to the descendants. Hence "sons and grandsons keep the sacrifice unbroken." The Wang Bi recension writes 輟, "interrupted," for the silk text's 絕, "cut off": one sense.

The cultivation of De has five ranks in file: from body to house to village to state to all-under-heaven. In the body it is genuine; in the house it runs to surplus; in the village it lasts long; in the state it is ample; in all-under-heaven it is broad. Five stages, each carried into the next — Laozi's order of near-to-far, small-to-great. The Great Learning: "from the Son of Heaven down to the common man, all alike take the cultivation of the body as root" — the same architecture. And Laozi raised this frame before the Great Learning did: the inner unity of pre-Qin learning shows itself here.

Yet the spirit of the five ranks parts Confucian from Daoist. The Confucian pushes outward from the self: "extend and fill it"; the Daoist reaches things from the self — the self-so spreads of its own accord. The Confucian extends presence from presence; the Daoist lets absence cover absence. So the Confucian comes near to extending oneself (推己), where Laozi says 觀, "behold" — beholding is not extending: one's own De is held up as a mirror, and the De out there shows itself in it. By the body behold bodies, by the house houses, by the village villages, by the state states, by all-under-heaven all-under-heaven: verifying the outer by the inner, the far by the near, like lit by like. To know all-under-heaven, one state suffices for the proof; to know a state, one village suffices; to know a village, one house suffices; to know a house, one body suffices. This is the teaching of being near to the Dao.

Weightiest: the silk text reads 亓德乃博, "its De grows broad," where Wang Bi puts 普, "universal." One graph, and the flavor parts. 博 is breadth of measure — abundance with room to spare; 普 is coverage — nothing left out. De spreads as rivers do, refusing no rivulet, as the sea declines no stream: wide of itself, everywhere without trying, so Laozi says 博. Wang Bi's 普 already leans toward the Confucian "extend it to all" — no longer Laozi's spreading-of-itself. The truth of the Dao lies in "taking the tending of the body as root, and governing state and house with what is left over": from the genuine to the broad, and the order must not be scrambled.

Last: "How do I know all-under-heaven is so? By this." What is "this"? The De of one's own body, nothing else. Knowing the world's order and disorder is no outward expedition but an inward verification. When the body's De is right, the house evens itself; the house even, the village concords of itself; the village concordant, the state orders itself; the state ordered, all-under-heaven levels itself. The sage knows all-under-heaven without stepping out the door, sees the Way of Heaven without peering through the window — the body for a mirror, De for the rule: Laozi's epistemology of politics at its most spare. The Doctrine of the Mean says "reach centered harmony, and heaven and earth take their places, the ten thousand things are nourished" — is this not the same word?
