The Book of De · Chapter Eight · Clear Stillness (請靚)

English Translation

The great completion seems chipped: its use does not wear out. The great fullness seems hollow: its use does not run out. The great straightness seems bent. The great skill seems clumsy. The great surplus seems a guttering flame (㶧).

Bustle defeats cold; stillness defeats heat. Clear stillness (請靚) can set all-under-heaven right.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The silk text of this chapter follows Manuscript A (MS A) throughout; Manuscript B (MS B) is badly damaged and can be compared only in the middle lines. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 大成若缺 / 亓用不幣: Follows MS A; lost in MS B. Wang Bi reads 其用不弊.
  • 大盈若𥁵: Follows MS A. The last graph is written with 水 and 盅; the original editors glossed it 沖 "hollow", Gao Ming 盅, and the Jicheng judges the gloss 沖 correct. MS B and the Peking University manuscript write 沖, the Fu Yi and Fan Yingyuan texts 盅. The small piece of MS B bearing 盈如沖亓 had been mounted in the wrong place; the Jicheng moves it down to its true position on the evidence of the mirror-image offset. (Jicheng IV, p. 13, n. 43; p. 199, n. 28)
  • 亓用不𡩫: Follows MS A. The original editors glossed the last graph 窘; Gao Ming took it as a variant form of 窮 "to run out", and the Jicheng follows him in glossing 窮. On the color plate its top is 宀, as in the 1980 transcription and Gao Ming's facsimile, whereas the Jicheng prints 穴; the plate is followed here. MS B reads 亓用不窮, where 用不窮, bracketed as a restoration, is confirmed in the Jicheng from the offset on the backing silk. The Guodian slips, the Peking University manuscript and the received texts all have 窮. (Jicheng IV, p. 13, n. 43; p. 199, n. 28)
  • 大直如詘: Follows MS A. MS B reads 大直如屈, placed after 大巧如拙: the first graph on a small fragment joined by Chen Jian is 出, the lower part of 屈, and 大直如 is read from the offset. The 1980 edition restored 大直如詘 after 其用不窮 on the model of the Fu Yi text, which the Jicheng rejects. MS B's order, skill – straightness – surplus, differs from MS A, the Peking University manuscript and all received texts. Wang Bi reads 大直若屈. (Jicheng IV, p. 14, n. 44; p. 199, n. 30)
  • 大巧如拙: In MS A, 巧 stands on a small fragment and is almost entirely lost. MS B's 大, bracketed as a restoration, is read directly in the Jicheng from the offset. Wang Bi reads 大巧若拙. (Jicheng IV, p. 14, n. 44; p. 199, n. 29)
  • 大贏如㶧: Follows MS A. The last graph has 火 as signific and 內 as phonetic; it was formerly misread as 炳. MS B reads 大𦀚如絀, its 大𦀚 read from a fragment joined by Chen Jian (keeping the component 呈) together with the offset, and taken as 大贏 "great surplus". MS A's graph and MS B's 絀 are interchangeable; Qiu Xigui reads them as 朒 "shortfall", as in the pair 贏朒 "surplus and shortfall". The Peking University manuscript reads 大盛如絀, the received texts 大辯若訥 "great eloquence seems halting". The original editors believed MS A had lost text here and restored MS B to four lines; the Jicheng, following Gao Ming, holds that neither silk manuscript ever had the line 大辯若訥. (Jicheng IV, p. 14, n. 44; p. 199, n. 31)
  • 趮勝寒: Follows MS A; 趮 is read as 躁 "agitation". For 寒 the Jicheng prints a miswritten form with 土 at the bottom, corrected as 〈寒〉. MS B reads 趮朕寒, with 朕 for 勝 "to defeat". Wang Bi reads 躁勝寒.
  • 靚勝炅: Follows MS A; 靚 is read as 靜 "stillness" and 炅 as 熱 "heat". Lost in MS B. Wang Bi reads 靜勝熱.
  • 請靚可以為天下正: Follows MS A; Wang Bi reads 清靜為天下正, without 可以. The line is damaged in MS B; the 1980 edition supplied 知 before 清靜 from the Fu Yi and Fan Yingyuan texts, and the Jicheng deletes it because MS A has no such word. (Jicheng IV, p. 199, n. 32)

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

This chapter lays out the five greatnesses that seem their opposites: the face of the utmost Dao, always contrary to common expectation. The silk text says the great completion seems chipped, its use does not wear out: one sense with Wang Bi's 不弊. The vessel most complete looks lacking from outside; complete yet seeming chipped, its use never wears. For what seems full must spill, and what seems whole must break. The great fullness seems hollow, its use never exhausted: full yet seeming hollow, so the use has no end; a vessel's usefulness lies in its hollow, not in its solid. This is the teaching that presence gives the advantage and absence gives the use. The great straightness seems bent (詘; Wang Bi writes 屈, the graphs interchanging): straightness at its utmost looks crooked. The great skill seems clumsy: skill at its utmost does not show its skill. The baldly straight get wounded; the visibly skillful get beaten. The sage is straight without display and skillful without show, not from guile but from following things to their end.

Then the silk text has 大贏如㶧, "the great surplus seems a guttering flame," where Wang Bi has 大辯若訥, "the great eloquence seems halting": the weightiest divergence between the two texts here. 㶧 is written 絀 in manuscript B, and Qiu Xigui reads it as the 朒 of 贏朒, "falling short": the utmost surplus looks from outside as though it fell short (or, read as written, 㶧 is the faintness of a flame, and the utmost abundance shows from outside as a low flicker). This is one pattern with "completion seems chipped" and "fullness seems hollow" — the utmost of a thing wears the face of its reverse. Wang Bi's "eloquence seems halting" swerves into the realm of speech, out of kind with the three lines before it. Eloquence is a matter of words; abundance is a matter of things; the chapter's fabric tears exactly there. Follow the silk text.

As for "bustle defeats cold, stillness defeats heat": movement to drive off cold, stillness to master heat. The silk text writes 請靚, which the 1980 editors read as 清靜, "clear stillness," as in Wang Bi. Alternatively, 請 is taken as a loan for 情, the genuine state, and 靚 is read as 靜, stillness. Joined, they say the Dao's genuine state lies in clear stillness: not a cultivated quality of quietness to be merely kept, but the Dao's own substance proving itself there. Wang Bi's "clear stillness sets all-under-heaven right" is plain and loses the resonance; the silk text makes it clear that stillness is not held in place by human effort; the Dao shows itself in it. That is its superiority. Heshang Gong, commenting on the highest-De chapter, says the sovereign of highest De governs by non-doing: the very point this chapter makes of bringing non-doing to the ordering of all-under-heaven.

Therefore keep to the chipped and do not fill it; dwell in the hollow and do not brim: this is how the Dao is great. The sage seeks it all in himself: he does not display his completion, does not flaunt his fullness, does not flourish his straightness; and completion, fullness, and straightness are all there within. Watch this chapter and you see that Laozi's learning is not in the outward form, but in turning the gaze back and lighting the inside.
