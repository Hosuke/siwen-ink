The Book of Dao · Chapter Four · The Dao in Use

English Translation

The Dao (道) is hollow (沖) — yet in use it never fills (有弗盈). Deep, ah (潚呵) — it seems (始, read as 似) the ancestor of the ten thousand things.

It blunts [its edge], unties its tangles (銼其解其紛: MS A drops a graph after 銼其; the bracketed words follow MS B's 銼亓兌), softens its shining, joins its dust.

Still and deep, ah (湛呵) — as if it were there. I do not know whose child it is: it seems to come before the August Lord (象帝之先).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The run from 潚呵始萬物之宗 to 和其光 and the last line follow Manuscript A (MS A); the first line and the run from 同亓塵 to 吾不知亓誰之子也 are damaged in MS A and are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 道沖而用之有弗盈也: MS A preserves only 盈也, and the Jicheng restores 道沖而用之又弗 from MS B; the line follows MS B, whose 有 the Jicheng reads as 又 "and yet". Wang Bi has 道沖而用之或不盈, with 或 "perhaps" for 有, 不 for 弗, and no final 也.
  • 潚呵始萬物之宗: Follows MS A. MS A writes 潚, read by the Jicheng as 淵 "abyss", which MS B writes; MS A writes 始 and MS B 佁 (given as 似 in Gao Ming's transcription; the plate shows 亻 beside 台), both read by the Jicheng as 似 "seems". Wang Bi has 淵兮似萬物之宗; where the silk text has the particle 呵, Wang Bi has 兮 throughout.
  • 銼其解其紛: Follows MS A; 銼 is read as 挫 "to blunt". The 1980 editors noted that MS B reads 銼其兌 and the received text 挫其銳, so that MS A has dropped a graph; the Jicheng supplies it in omission brackets, the graph being absent from the manuscript. MS B reads 銼亓兌，解亓芬, with 兌 read as 銳 "sharp point" and 芬 as 紛 "tangle". (Jicheng IV, p. 45, n. 21)
  • 和其光: Follows MS A; MS B reads 和亓光. In the Dao section MS A writes 其, MS B 亓.
  • 同亓塵: MS A preserves only 同; the line follows MS B. For 塵 "dust" the Jicheng prints a form of reduced 麤 over 土, which has no Unicode code point and is given here as 塵; the graph before it is 亓 in the manuscript, formerly transcribed 其 and now restored after the Jicheng.
  • 湛呵佁或存: MS A preserves only 或存; the line follows MS B. 佁 is read as 似 "seems"; Gao Ming's transcription gives 似, while the plate shows 亻 beside 台. Wang Bi has 湛兮似或存.
  • 吾不知亓誰之子也: Follows MS B. MS A reads 吾不知□子也, in which the Jicheng restores the single graph 誰, so that MS A lacks both 其 and 之. Wang Bi has 吾不知誰之子, without 亓 and 也.
  • 象帝之先: The manuscripts agree, as does Wang Bi.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

This chapter of the Book of Dao speaks of the Dao in use. The Dao is that by which the ten thousand things are so — not to be sought in shapes and tracks — and so it opens with "the Dao is hollow." 沖 is emptiness: the Dao takes emptiness for its substance, and so it holds the ten thousand things and is never drawn dry. The silk text says 道沖而用之有弗盈也 — "in use it never fills" — where Wang Bi has 或不盈, "it seems not to brim": 或 exchanged for 有, and the sense tightens: that the Dao's use never brims is its nature, not its luck. The Dao is high beyond height, deep past sounding — this same inexhaustible emptiness. The Dao's substance is like an empty vessel: fill it to make solidity, and the solid comes and spills back out; hollow in use, it never brims again — inexhaustibility at its limit. So the sage patterns on it: diminish and diminish again, down to non-doing — and nothing left undone.

"Deep-sunken, it seems the ancestor of the ten thousand things": 潚 and Wang Bi's 淵 are near in sound and one in sense, the deep basin of the Dao's substance. Ancestor here is not ruler but root-spring: all things come out of the Dao, so the Dao is their ancestor. The Zhuangzi: "the Dao — there is feeling in it and faithfulness; no doing and no form" — the very sense of ancestor-of-the-ten-thousand-things.

"Blunts the sharp, unties the tangle, softens the shining, joins the dust" — four works, one principle. Wang Bi's note is fine: "blunted, and nothing lost; untangled, and no labor; light softened, and no stain; its body joined to dust, and its truth unchanged." Blunting the sharp is not baring one's edge; untying the tangle is not wading into the quarrel; softening the shining is gathering the blaze within; joining the dust is walking unmarked in the crowd. This is the Dao's way of holding itself — and the sage's art of hiding. Laozi writes the Dao here, and writes the sage too: the Dao does not flash its edge, and the sage does not flaunt the Dao. To keep to the soft and weak — this.

"Still and deep — seeming to be, and not to be": 湛 is immersion, deep and fine beyond sounding. The three words 似或存 (the silk text writes 佁, read as 似) are the most subtle in the chapter: not definitely there, not definitely not. The Dao is emptiest and realest — neither presence nor absence — and so it seems to be, without a fixed place of being. The Book of Changes: "the spirit has no direction; the Change has no body" — one light with this.

"I do not know whose child it is": the Dao is born before heaven and earth — meaning the Dao admits no comparison with any August Lord of later ages. This line is the chapter's peak: the Dao is before the Lord-on-high. 帝 is the old word for a willing, ruling Heaven; and if the Dao precedes even 帝, then the willing Heaven too is born of the Dao. The law of the ten thousand things issues from no will above — it is the self-so of things themselves. Here is the taproot of China's arts of the Dao paying homage to no personal god — and the deep parting from other houses.

In sum: emptiness for ancestor, never-brimming for use, blunting-and-untying for working, softening-and-joining for conduct, still-as-if-there for substance, before-the-August-Lord for the peak. The sage who embodies this knows the way of advance and retreat, survival and ruin. The world drowns in contrivance and knows nothing of the subtlety of the empty and absent — like ladling water until it spills: they can never stay hollow and ready for use. A pity.
