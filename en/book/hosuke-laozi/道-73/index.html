The Book of Dao · Chapter Twenty-nine · The Self-so

English Translation

Would you take all-under-heaven and do at it (取天下而為之)? I see it: it cannot be forced (其弗得巳).

All-under-heaven is a spirit-vessel (神器): not a thing to be done at.

Who does at it undoes it; who grips it loses it.

Things: some go ahead, some follow; some are hot (熱), some are broken (䂳, read as 挫 — MS B drops 或吹或強, "some blow, some stand strong," between them); some are built up, some fall.

Hence the sage drops the extreme, drops the inflated, drops the lavish (去甚，去大，去楮).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The chapter largely follows Manuscript A (MS A); 夫天下神器也 and 或熱或䂳 follow Manuscript B (MS B), and 吾見其弗得巳 combines the surviving portions of both. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 吾見其弗得巳: MS A preserves 吾見其弗, the rest lost; MS B preserves 得巳, the beginning lost; the line combines the two. 巳 is read as 已. Wang Bi has 吾見其不得已.
  • 夫天下神器也: In MS A 夫天下 is lost, while 神 is read directly by the Jicheng; the line follows MS B. Wang Bi has 天下神器, without 夫 or 也.
  • 非可為者也: The manuscripts agree; Wang Bi has 不可為也.
  • 為者敗之 / 執者失之: Follows MS A; MS B reads 為之者敗之，執之者失之, each with an extra 之. Wang Bi agrees with MS A.
  • 物或行或遀: Follows MS A. MS A writes 遀, MS B 隋, both read as 隨 "to follow". Above 物 the MS B scribe first miswrote 或, then struck it out; older transcriptions that read 故物 here have supplied 故 from the received text. Wang Bi has 故物或行或隨. (Jicheng IV, p. 210, n. 47)
  • 或熱或䂳: MS A reads 或炅或【吹，或強或挫】, with 炅 read as 熱 "hot", and is broken after the second 或. MS B reads 或熱或䂳, having dropped the four graphs 或吹或強; the 1980 editors noted that these could be restored after the Fu Yi and Fan Yingyuan texts, and the Jicheng supplies them as an omission on the evidence of the Peking University manuscript. MS B's 或熱 thus corresponds to MS A's 或炅, and its 或䂳 to MS A's lost 或挫. MS A has lost 吹，或強或挫 here; the text follows MS B, and the two clauses MS B drops are not supplied. For MS B's 䂳 the 1980 note has 硻, and Gao Ming's transcription 石 beside 垂. Wang Bi reads 或歔或吹，或強或羸. (Jicheng IV, p. 210, n. 48)
  • 或坏或撱: Follows MS A, whose 坏 and 撱 the Jicheng reads as 培 "to bank up" and 墮 "to fall"; Gao Ming's transcription gives 杯 for 坏. MS B reads 或陪或𡐦. Wang Bi has 或挫或隳, differing from the silk text.
  • 是以聲人去甚: Follows MS A, with 聲 read as 聖 "sage"; MS B writes 𦔻 (耳 beside 口).
  • 去大 / 去楮: Follows MS A. 大 is read as 泰 "inflated", 楮 as 奢 "lavish"; MS B writes 諸 for 楮. The silk text has the order 甚, 大, 楮; Wang Bi reads 去甚，去奢，去泰.

Commentary

Yaomiao says: the vessel called all-under-heaven is a spirit-vessel — nothing more. A spirit-vessel runs by its own law: not to be held by craft, not to be steered by contrivance. The silk text says 將欲取天下而為之，吾見其弗得巳 — "would you take all-under-heaven and do at it? I see it: it cannot be forced" — steeper than Wang Bi's 吾見其不得已, "I see it cannot be helped." 不得已 is the passive: circumstances do not allow it. 弗得已 is the active verdict: knowing it cannot be done and doing at it anyway — the wording of self-undoing. One graph between them, and the weight hangs far apart; the reader must not miss it. "Who does at it undoes it; who grips it loses it" — these eight words are the guideline of the whole Laozi, and they answer, from the other side, the twenty-seventh chapter's "assisting the self-so of the ten thousand things." All-under-heaven never needed to be governed by human doing; to govern it by doing is to fish in the deep and to pick a tree's growth with your hands.

So the sage, knowing this, drops the extreme, drops the inflated, drops the lavish. The silk text's 楮 borrows for 奢, lavish — one sense with Wang Bi's 去奢, different graph. The extreme is going past; the inflated is puffing up; the lavish is over-ornament. The three droppings drop the private contrivance and give back the root of the self-so. The Book of Changes: "no thought, no doing — still and unmoving; stirred, it goes through all-under-heaven's every turn" — just this. He who governs all-under-heaven cannot but pattern on heaven. The Way of Heaven: not contending, and good at winning; not speaking, and good at answering — its work is simply the self-so. The sage embodies heaven and walks: drop the private self, and all-under-heaven sets itself straight with nothing needing to be done in between — the fine point of the Dao and De, and a mirror for ten thousand ages.
