The Book of De · Chapter Twenty-eight · Dark De

English Translation

Those of old who worked the Dao (故曰, a miswriting of 古之, as in MS B) did not use it to make the people clever (明); they would use it to make them simple (愚). The people are hard to govern: because of what they know (知).

So to govern the state by knowing is the state's bandit; to govern the state by not-knowing is the state's De.

To know these two constantly is itself the rule (稽式). To know the rule constantly: this is called dark De (玄德).

Dark De is deep, is far; it runs counter to things — and then it reaches the great accord (大順).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

This chapter follows Manuscript A (MS A); 夫民之難治也, 國之德也 and 乃至大順, damaged in MS A, are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 故曰為道者非以明民也: Follows MS A; MS B reads 古之為道者非以眀□□, writing 明 with 目 as 眀, with 民也 lost. The original editors note that other texts have 古之 for 故曰, and Gao Ming regards MS A as a copying error; the Jicheng compares the opening of Wang Bi's chapter 68, where many texts have 古之善為士者不武 and MS B corrupts 古之 into 故. Received texts have 善 before 為道, which MS A, MS B and the Peking University manuscript lack. Wang Bi reads 古之善為道者，非以明民. (Jicheng IV, p. 33, n. 141)
  • 將以愚之也: Both manuscripts agree. In MS B 將以 is lost, and 愚, a restoration in the 1980 edition, is read directly by the Jicheng from a fragment stuck to the backing silk. Wang Bi lacks 也. (Jicheng IV, p. 203, n. 127)
  • 夫民之難治也: Follows MS B; MS A lacks 夫 and loses 治 (its 也 is read directly by the Jicheng).
  • 以亓知也: Both manuscripts agree, as does the Peking University manuscript. The Jicheng reads 知 as 智 "cleverness", but notes that since some early texts lack 多 here, 知 might also be taken in its own sense, "knowing". Wang Bi reads 以其智多. (Jicheng IV, p. 33, n. 142)
  • 故以知知邦 / 邦之賊也: MS A writes 故以知﹦邦﹦之賊也, with repetition marks after both 知 and 邦, which the Jicheng reads 故以智知邦，邦之賊也 "so to know the state by cleverness is the state's bandit"; MS B is the same but with 國 for 邦 and 戝 for 賊. Wang Bi reads 故以智治國，國之賊.
  • 以不知知邦 / 國之德也: MS A writes 以不知﹦邦﹦之德也, with the repetition mark after 邦 and the graph 之 lost; MS B writes 以不知﹦國﹦之德也, which the text follows here, hence 國. MS B and the Peking University manuscript have 知國 for 知邦, the received texts 治國. Wang Bi reads 不以智治國，國之福, with 不 before 以 and 福 "blessing" for 德; the Suizhou text, several Dunhuang manuscripts and the quotation in the Wenzi ("Daoyuan") have 德, agreeing with the silk. (Jicheng IV, p. 33, n. 143)
  • 恒知此兩者亦稽式也: Follows MS A; MS B writes 恆 for 恒. MS B's 稽 was read simply as 稽 in the 1980 edition; the Jicheng transcribes it strictly with 甘 in place of 旨, and Chen Jian points to Qin seals that write 稽 with 甘. The Peking University manuscript has 楷式; received texts vary between 楷式 and 稽式, and Jiang Xichang and Zhu Qianzhi take 稽式 as 楷式 "pattern, model". Wang Bi reads 知此兩者亦稽式, without 恒 and 也. (Jicheng IV, p. 33, n. 144; p. 203, n. 128)
  • 恒知稽式: Both manuscripts agree (MS B with 恆); Wang Bi has 常知稽式.
  • 此胃玄德 / 玄德深矣: MS A writes 此胃玄﹦德﹦深矣, with repetition marks after both 玄 and 德, to be read 此胃玄德，玄德深矣; MS B has 是 for 此. 胃 is read as 謂 "to call".
  • 與物反矣: Follows MS A, whose 反 survives on the silk and is read directly by the Jicheng. In MS B 與 is lost and 矣 is 也.
  • 乃至大順: MS A preserves only 乃; follows MS B. Wang Bi reads 然後乃至大順.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The work of working the Dao is to diminish and diminish again, down to non-doing. What this chapter speaks is exactly the learning of diminishing knowledge.

"Making them simple" does not mean making the people dull. The highest De has no name, and the sage's governing of the world is the same. Foreknowing is presumption and guesswork, taking one's own narrow view for heaven's vastness — the flower of the Dao and the beginning of folly. When the people are habituated to it, they are hard to govern. The sage wants them to return to unhewn wood, so he makes them simple: simple is unhewn, not stupid. The Great Learning's "rectifying the heart" and "sincerifying the intent": a heart right and an intent true, unscreened by appetite — come to the same point.

Manuscript A says 以知知邦，邦之賊；以不知知邦，邦之德 (manuscript B, avoiding a taboo, writes 國). The Wang Bi recension: 以智治國，國之賊；不以智治國，國之福. One graph apart, and the senses stand a thousand miles off. 知 (the Jicheng reads it as 智, adding that it may also be taken as it stands; here it is taken as it stands) is the knowing that distinguishes: where there is knowing, there is right and wrong, profit and harm; each man brandishes his knowing, and contention rises. 智 tilts toward craft and cunning. And that is what makes a governor the bandit of the state. Again: the silk text says 邦, state in the wide sense that embraces heaven, earth, people, and things; Wang Bi's 國 narrows it to the polity.

The passage on the measuring-rule: he who can constantly know that governing by knowing is the bandit and governing by not-knowing is the De, holds the root law of rule. Heshang Gong's note says "玄 is heaven; this De is one with heaven" — truly. Manuscript A opens with 故曰, "hence it is said"; manuscript B has 古之為道者, "of old, those who worked the Dao"; Wang Bi has 古之善為道者, "of old, those good at working the Dao." Manuscript A's 故曰 the original editors and Gao Ming alike take for a copyist's slip, and the Jicheng compares other cases of 古之 corrupted into 故, so the reading should follow manuscript B's 古之. Yet neither silk manuscript has 善: they say plainly "those who worked the Dao," not sorting them into good and not good — steeper and more direct than Wang Bi, and this is what makes the silk text precious.

Dark De: deep, far, counter to things — and then the great accord. The world chases cleverness and runs with things; dark De turns against the current. At the end of that turning, it joins De with the Dao itself: close to what the Commentary on the Changes calls "going before heaven, and heaven not opposing it." Turned, and then in accord with the ten thousand things: that is the great accord. That is dark De.
