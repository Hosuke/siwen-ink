The Book of De · Chapter Three · Hearing the Dao

English Translation

The finest student of the Way hears the Dao (道) and strains to walk it (堇, with toil, and barely). The middling student hears the Dao: now it is there, now it is gone. The lowest student hears the Dao and laughs out loud. Were it not laughed at, it would not suffice to be the Dao.

Hence the Established Sayings have it: The bright way seems spent. The advancing way seems a retreat. The level way seems knotted. The highest De (德) seems bathing water. Great whiteness seems soiled. Broad De seems lacking. Founded De seems shirking. The genuine seems to shift. The great square has no corners. The great vessel is exempt from completion. The great note is faint of sound. Heaven's image has no form. The Dao, vast, has no name.

Only the Dao is good at beginning and good at completing.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

This chapter sets out the three grades of hearing the Dao, then lets the Established Sayings unfold the images of highest De. Set the silk text against the Wang Bi recension and six differences matter.

First, 堇 against 勤, "diligent." Anciently 堇 passes for 勤, but it also holds 僅, "barely": the finest student can barely walk it, and walks it with toil; this is more than diligence. Second, 明道如費 against 若昧, "seems dim." 費 is expenditure; 昧 is darkness. Dim merely pairs off against bright; but 費, bright yet seeming spent, seeming spent yet in fact gaining, cuts deeper. Third, 上德如浴 against 若谷: 浴 holds the power of washing; 谷 is only the vessel that receives. Fourth, the weightiest: 大器免成 against 晚成, "completed late." Exempt-from-completion borrows no apparatus, and matches the non-doing of highest De; completed-late still stands inside time, while exempt-from-completion has stepped outside it. Fifth, 道殷无名 against 隱无名, "hidden without name." 殷 is vastness: the Dao is too vast to be named, a grander sense than mere hiding. Sixth, 善始且善成 against 善貸且成: one thread from beginning to end, tighter than the received text's lending-and-completing.

The sovereign of highest De empties his heart, lets things be, and answers without fixed form; the work is done, the matter complete, and no one knows whose doing it was: thus, "without an end in view." The finest student hears and walks precisely because he knows these "seems" are no deliberate dazzle: they are how the Dao actually looks.

The lowest student's laughter and the finest student's toil are of a piece; both are the Dao's inverted images. The bright way seems spent, the advancing way seems a retreat, the level way seems knotted: every "seems" of the Established Sayings looks contrary and is exact. The great square without corners, the great note faint of sound, heaven's image without form: this is the way of the utmost, reverting.

The Dao runs against the common grain; what the common laugh at is exactly what makes it the Dao. The silk text's 免 and 殷: in each a single graph is swapped, and the whole point shifts. Learn to weigh the depth of meaning inside such variant graphs, and you may begin to read Laozi.
