The Book of De · Chapter Three · Hearing the Dao

English Translation

The highest […] the Dao (道) and strains to walk it (堇, with toil, and barely). The middling student hears the Dao: now it is there, now it is gone. The lowest student hears the Dao and laughs out loud. Not laughed at, it […] to be the Dao.

Hence the Established Sayings have it: The bright way seems spent. The advancing way seems a retreat. The level way seems knotted. The highest De (德) seems bathing water. Great whiteness seems soiled. Broad De seems lacking. Founded De seems shirking. Plain substance […]. The great square has no corners. The great vessel is exempt from completion. The great note is faint of sound. Heaven's image has no form. The Dao, vast, has no name.

Only the Dao is good at beginning and good at completing.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The lines of Manuscript A (MS A) that carried this chapter are almost entirely lost, so the silk text follows Manuscript B (MS B) throughout, keeping its lacunae where it too is damaged. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 上□□道: Lines 9 and 10 of MS A, which carried this chapter, are wholly lost; the 1980 edition filled them from MS B and the received text at thirty-two graphs per line. The Jicheng replaces all of this with marks of indeterminate loss, except for the graphs on a small fragment spanning two lines newly joined by Chen Jian; its right column keeps the 辵 component of a graph, evidently the 道 of this line. In MS B the two graphs after 上 are lost; Wang Bi reads 上士聞道. (Jicheng IV, p. 10, n. 23)
  • 堇能行之: MS B writes 堇; Wang Bi reads 勤而行之 "strives and walks it".
  • 大芺之 / 弗芺□□以為道: The Jicheng reads both instances of MS B's 芺 as 笑 "to laugh"; the two graphs after 弗芺 are lost. Wang Bi reads 大笑之 and 不笑不足以為道.
  • 是以建言有之曰: Follows MS B. The left column of the new MS A fragment keeps 之 and 眀: 之 is the last graph of this line, and its position shows that MS A had no 曰 after 建言有之. The Peking University manuscript likewise lacks 曰, and the Guodian slips read 是以建言又之. Wang Bi reads 故建言有之. (Jicheng IV, p. 10, n. 23)
  • 眀道如費: MS B writes 眀 with the eye component, read as 明 "bright"; MS A keeps only this first graph 眀, on the new fragment. Wang Bi reads 明道若昧; throughout the following lines MS B has 如 where Wang Bi has 若.
  • 進道如退: For 退 in MS B the Jicheng prints a variant form with the 彳 component, read as 退.
  • 夷道如類 / 上德如浴: Wang Bi reads 夷道若纇 and 上德若谷.
  • 建德如揄: 揄 was treated as a lacuna in the 1980 edition and restored as 偷 from the received text; the Jicheng reads 揄 directly from the mirror-image offset on the backing silk, glossing it 偷. Wang Bi reads 建德若偷. (Jicheng IV, p. 198, n. 12)
  • 質□□□: In MS B the three graphs after 質 are lost. The last keeps two strokes at its lower right, the remains of 俞 or of a graph with 俞 as a component; the 1980 edition restored 渝, which the Jicheng changes to □（渝）. Wang Bi reads 質真若渝. (Jicheng IV, p. 198, n. 13)
  • 大方无禺 / 大器免成: Wang Bi reads 大方無隅 and 大器晚成.
  • 天象无刑: 刑 is read as 形 "form"; Wang Bi reads 大象無形.
  • 道褱无名: The lower half of the graph after 道 is lost. The 1980 edition read it as a variant of 褎; the Jicheng, following Chen Jian, holds that the form is in fact 段, which in Qin and Han writing was confused with 殷, and prints 段〈殷〉 "vast". What survives of the upper half cannot settle the matter, so 褱 is provisionally kept here. Wang Bi reads 道隱無名. (Jicheng IV, p. 198, n. 16)
  • 夫唯道 / 善始且善成: Follows MS B. Line 11 of MS A is also mostly lost, leaving only a small fragment with 道 and the 善 below it; the Jicheng keeps the 1980 restorations 夫唯 and 始且 and replaces the rest with marks of loss. Wang Bi reads 夫唯道，善貸且成. (Jicheng IV, p. 10, n. 24)

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

This chapter sets out the three grades of hearing the Dao, then lets the Established Sayings unfold the images of highest De. Set the silk text against the Wang Bi recension and six differences matter.

First, 堇 against 勤, "diligent." Anciently 堇 passes for 勤, but it also holds 僅, "barely": the finest student can barely walk it, and walks it with toil; this is more than diligence. Second, 眀道如費 against 若昧, "seems dim." 費 is expenditure; 昧 is darkness. Dim merely pairs off against bright; but 費, bright yet seeming spent, seeming spent yet in fact gaining, cuts deeper. Third, 上德如浴 against 若谷: 浴 holds the power of washing; 谷 is only the vessel that receives. Fourth, the weightiest: 大器免成 against 晚成, "completed late." Exempt-from-completion borrows no apparatus, and matches the non-doing of highest De; completed-late still stands inside time, while exempt-from-completion has stepped outside it. Fifth, 道褱无名 against 隱无名, "hidden without name." The lower half of the silk graph is worn away; it is kept here in the form 褱, while the Jicheng, following Chen Jian, takes it as 段, a Qin–Han confusion for 殷. If 殷, it is vastness — the Dao is too vast to be named, a grander sense than mere hiding; if the form is kept, 褱 reads as 懷, "to embrace": the Dao holds all things in its embrace and names none of them — this too makes sense. The two readings are not settled, but both make the Dao's namelessness come from its greatness and fullness, not from its hiding. Sixth, 善始且善成 against 善貸且成: one thread from beginning to end, tighter than the received text's lending-and-completing.

The sovereign of highest De empties his heart, lets things be, and answers without fixed form; the work is done, the matter complete, and no one knows whose doing it was: thus, "without an end in view." The finest student hears and walks precisely because he knows these "seems" are no deliberate dazzle: they are how the Dao actually looks.

The lowest student's laughter and the finest student's toil are of a piece; both are the Dao's inverted images. The bright way seems spent, the advancing way seems a retreat, the level way seems knotted (類, not Wang Bi's 纇): every "seems" of the Established Sayings looks contrary and is exact. The great square without corners (禺, not Wang Bi's 隅), the great note faint of sound, heaven's image without form (刑, not Wang Bi's 形): this is the way of the utmost, reverting.

The Dao runs against the common grain; what the common laugh at is exactly what makes it the Dao. The silk text's 免 and 褱 (殷): in each a single graph is swapped, and the whole point shifts. Learn to weigh the depth of meaning inside such variant graphs, and you may begin to read Laozi.
