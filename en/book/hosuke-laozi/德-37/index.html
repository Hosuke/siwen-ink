The Book of De · Chapter Thirty-seven · Fearing What Is to Be Feared

English Translation

When the people do not fear what is to be feared (畏畏), the great fear is on its way.

Do not bar their dwellings (母閘亓所居); do not oppress their livelihood (毋猒亓所生). Only by not oppressing them is one not loathed.

Hence the sage knows himself, but does not show himself; cherishes himself, but does not exalt himself. He leaves that and takes this.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The first two lines are lost in Manuscript A (MS A) and follow Manuscript B (MS B); the lines from 母閘亓所居 to 夫唯弗猒 follow MS A; the three lines from 是以不猒 on are lost in MS A and are supplied from MS B; the last line returns to MS A. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 民之不畏畏: MS A preserves only the final 畏﹦; the rest is supplied from MS B. In both manuscripts 畏 carries a repetition mark and is read 畏畏; for MS A the Jicheng glosses it as 畏威. The Peking University manuscript and the received texts all lack 之; Wang Bi reads 民不畏威. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 177)
  • 則大畏將至矣: In MS A 畏將 is lost and is supplied from MS B; 至 is read directly by the Jicheng. Wang Bi reads 則大威至.
  • 母閘亓所居: MS A places a chapter-division dot before this line, apparently assigning 民之不畏畏，則大畏將至矣 to the preceding chapter. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 177)
  • 母閘亓所居: Follows MS A. 母 is read as 毋 "do not", and 閘 as 狎. MS B reads 毋𠇺亓所居; the Jicheng transcribes the graph as 𠇺 (人 beside 甲), likewise read as 狎. There is a hole in the silk here, and only the 人 component and a vertical stroke survive. Wang Bi reads 無狎其所居.
  • 毋猒亓所生 / 夫唯弗猒: Both manuscripts agree; Wang Bi reads 無厭其所生，夫唯不厭.
  • 是以不猒: MS A preserves only 是; the rest is supplied from MS B.
  • 是以𦔻人自知而不自見也: Lost entirely in MS A and taken from MS B, hence 𦔻 for 聖. Wang Bi reads 是以聖人自知不自見, without 而 or 也.
  • 自愛而不自貴也: In MS A the first 自 is lost and is supplied from MS B; 愛 is read directly by the Jicheng. Wang Bi reads 自愛不自貴.
  • 故去被取此: Follows MS A; the original editors note that these five graphs were struck through in red. MS B reads 故去罷而取此. Both 被 and 罷 are loans for 彼 "that"; Wang Bi reads 故去彼取此. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 178)

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

Chapter thirty-seven of the Book of De — this chapter is titled "Fearing What Is to Be Feared." Its point cuts deep: the utmost warning for those who govern.

Set against the Wang Bi recension, there are three differences; here is what matters in them.

First, 民之不畏畏: Wang Bi writes 民不畏威, "when the people do not fear the majesty." The Jicheng reads the second 畏 as 威, and then the silk text and Wang Bi agree in sense; but both silk manuscripts write 畏畏, and Yaomiao ventures that it may be read as it stands — 畏畏 is to hold in awe what ought to be held in awe — the self-so order of the Way of Heaven, of rightness and pattern. 威 is external force: majesty, punishment. Read as it stands, the silk text says: when the people lose their inward awe, the great fear arrives. Wang Bi says: when the people do not fear penal force, a greater force comes. The depths plainly part: the silk text is a doctrine of the moral self-so; Wang Bi, a calculus of rule. This fork in Laozi's root meaning must not be blurred.

Second, 母閘亓所居 (manuscript A; 母 read as 毋): Wang Bi writes 無狎其所居. Manuscript B writes 𠇺, 人 beside 甲, and the 1980 editors and the Jicheng alike read both 閘 and 𠇺 as 狎 — then the silk text and Wang Bi agree in sense. 狎 is to grow familiar, to take liberties with, to treat lightly — do not slight the people's dwellings. Yaomiao ventures that manuscript A's 閘 may be read as it stands: 閘 is to bar, to block up — the ruler is not to encroach on the people's land or block up their homes, but to let them have a settled place; concrete government and abstract admonition, then, light each other up. The Heshang Gong text reads 無狹其所居; 狹, too, means to hem in and constrict, and may answer to the reading of 閘 as it stands.

Third, "knows himself, but does not show himself" (自知而不自見也, manuscript B; manuscript A is damaged): Wang Bi lacks both 而 and 也. To show oneself (自見) is to flaunt outward; to know oneself is the light turned within. To exalt oneself (自貴) is to set oneself above others; to cherish oneself (自愛) is to hold oneself dear without pride. Four in file: the governor first drops the private itch to shine and dominate — only then can he reach the people.

The chapter's crux: "Only by not oppressing them is one not loathed." When those above do not oppress the people's life, the people do not loathe the rule above; high and low at peace with each other — the goodness of non-doing. Reverse it: press and harry the people with majesty and punishment until they have no settled dwelling and no work to love, and the people lose their awe — and the great fear arrives.

Seen from here, the sage's governing lies not in the sternness of majesty but in letting the people each be settled in their homes and fulfilled in their lives — governing his own self first, and only then reaching the people. This is Laozi's root intent, and it is the silk text alone that keeps it true.
