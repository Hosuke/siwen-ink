The Book of De · Chapter Twenty-four · Dwelling Low

English Translation

A great state is the downstream — the female of all-under-heaven, the meeting-water (郊, read 交) of all-under-heaven.

The female constantly defeats the male by stillness (靜); being still, she properly stays low.

A great state, lowering itself to a small state, takes the small state; a small state, lowering itself to a great state, is taken in by the great state. So the one lowers itself in order to take; the other lowers itself and is taken in.

The great state wants no more than to gather and feed men; the small state wants no more than to enter and serve.

Both get what they want — then it is the great that should stay low.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

This chapter largely follows Manuscript A (MS A); four lines lost in MS A — 牝恆以靜, 故大國以下□國, 故大國者不過欲并畜人 and 則大者宜為下 — are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B), which is why 邦 and 國 alternate in the text. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 大邦者下流也: Follows MS A. MS B writes 國 for 邦 throughout, presumably avoiding the personal name of the Han founder, Liu Bang; so below. In MS B 流 is lost; its 者, bracketed as a restoration in the 1980 edition, the Jicheng reads directly from the mirror-image offset on the backing silk. In MS A the right side of 牝 carries an extra stroke, a corrupted form; the Jicheng does not change the reading. (Jicheng IV, p. 32, n. 122; p. 202, n. 108)
  • 天下之牝 / 天下之郊也: Follows MS A; MS B reads 天下之牝也，天下之交也. MS B, the Peking University manuscript and most received texts have 交, but the Suizhou text, several Dunhuang manuscripts and Cheng Xuanying's text also have 郊. The Jicheng follows the original editors in reading 郊 as 交 "meeting, union" and, with Gao Ming, takes 天下之郊（交）也，牝恆以靜勝牡 as one sentence, an image drawn from the mating of female and male; yet since no other text borrows 郊 for 交, it does not rule out that MS A read 大邦者下流也，天下之牝，天下之郊也 as a single sentence. MS B's 下, 也 and 天下之 were restorations in the 1980 edition and are read directly from the offset in the Jicheng. Wang Bi reverses the two lines: 天下之交，天下之牝. (Jicheng IV, p. 32, n. 123; p. 202, nn. 109, 110)
  • 牝恆以靜朕牡為其靜也故宜為下也: MS A reads 牝恒以靚勝牡，為亓靚【也，故】宜為下, with 也故 lost, so the line follows MS B. MS A uses 靚 and 勝, MS B 靜 and 朕; 朕 is read as 勝 "to overcome". Wang Bi has 牝常以靜勝牡，以靜為下. The original editors' note observes that the received text reads 以靜為下, the Peking University manuscript 以其靜也，故為下, and Fu Yi 以其靖，故為下也, and that the two silk texts stand close to one another. (Jicheng IV, p. 202, n. 111)
  • 故大國以下□國: Damaged in both. MS B reads 故大國以下□國 with 小 lost; MS A reads 大邦以下小【邦】, lacking 故 but preserving 小 (its 以 is read directly by the Jicheng). The line follows MS B, whose gap MS A shows to be 小.
  • 則取小邦 / 小邦以下大邦: MS A writes 則取小﹦邦﹦以下大邦, with repetition marks after 小 and 邦, to be read 則取小邦，小邦以下大邦; MS B is the same with 國.
  • 則取於大邦: Both manuscripts agree (MS B with 國); Wang Bi lacks 於: 則取大國.
  • 故或下以取 / 或下而取: Follows MS A; in MS B the second 或 is lost. MS B's 以取, restored in the 1980 edition across the upper and lower halves of the line, is read directly from the offset at line 23, upper half, in the Jicheng. (Jicheng IV, p. 203, n. 112)
  • 故大國者不過欲并畜人: MS A loses its first graph (□大邦者不過欲兼畜人), so the line follows MS B. MS A has 兼, MS B 并; Wang Bi has 兼 and lacks 故 and 者. MS B's 過, a restoration in the 1980 edition, is read directly from the offset in the Jicheng. (Jicheng IV, p. 203, n. 113)
  • 小邦者不過欲入事人: Follows MS A; MS B reads 小國不【過】欲入事人, without 者, its 過 lost and restored in the Jicheng.
  • 夫皆得亓欲: Both manuscripts agree; MS B's 皆得, a restoration in the 1980 edition, is read directly from the offset in the Jicheng. Wang Bi has 夫兩者各得其所欲. (Jicheng IV, p. 203, n. 114)
  • 則大者宜為下: MS A preserves only 為下, so the line follows MS B. The Jicheng restores MS A as 【故大邦者宜】為下, five graphs, differing from MS B; the restoration is the editors' estimate. Wang Bi reads 大者宜為下, without 則.

Commentary

In this chapter Laozi takes water as the figure for the dealings of states, and the whole way of great and small states together is one word: low. Water's nature runs down and does not contend; the rivers flow east to go home to the sea; the ravine-streams race to the hollow. Let a great state be the downstream of all-under-heaven, and the ten thousand states come home to it — is that chance?

The female of all-under-heaven, the meeting-water of all-under-heaven: 牝 is the female — still, and by stillness defeating the male. A great state like the female does not trample the small by force; it summons by stillness and takes by lying low. "The female constantly defeats the male by 靚": manuscript A writes 靚, read as 靜, stillness; manuscript B and the Wang Bi text write 靜 outright — differing graphs, one sense. Stillness is no cowardice — still, and therefore able to receive; set below, it defeats what is above — the self-so of the Dao. When the great state humbles itself, the small states attach themselves of their own accord — just so.

A great state lowering itself takes the small; a small state lowering itself is taken in by the great; each gets its wish. The silk text says 或下以取，或下而取 — one lowers in order to take, one lowers and is taken. 以取 is the great state's initiative, gathering and feeding men; 而取 is the small state's reception, entering and serving. A distinction carried in the syntax itself — exactly where the old writers' precision shows.

The close: both get their wish; then the great should stay low. Both are satisfied, but the burden lies on the great. Why? The high, from on high, find going low hard; the low, low already, find it easy. A great state stands high — that it can lower itself is what makes it worthy; a small state is low already — its lowering is nothing remarkable. Hence "the great should stay low": no excess of modesty in the sage, but the deep self-knowledge of one who knows the Dao and knows his seat. Water benefits the ten thousand things and keeps still, dwelling where the crowd loathes to be — is not the key to a thousand ages of statecraft right here?
