The Book of Dao · Chapter One · Beholding the Dao

English Translation

The Dao (道) that can be spoken of is not the constant Dao.

The name (名) that can be named is not the constant name.

The nameless is the beginning of the ten thousand things. The named is the mother of the ten thousand things.

Hence: constantly without desire — to behold its subtlety (眇). Constantly with desire — to behold where it runs to its bounds (噭, read as 徼).

These two issue from the same source; different names, one calling. Dark and dark again (玄之有玄) — the gate of all subtleties.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The whole chapter follows Manuscript A (MS A). The opening of Manuscript B (MS B) is damaged: the run from 非恒道也 to 名可名也 and the line 以觀其眇 are lost there. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 道可道也 / 非恒道也 / 名可名也 / 非恒名也: Follow MS A. At the head of MS B a black square once stood before 道; it has since broken away, and the Jicheng restores it inside lacuna brackets. In MS B the nine graphs 非恒道也名可名也非 are lost, leaving only 道可道也 and 恆名也. In MS A all four short clauses end in 也, and MS B, damaged as it is, shows the same; the received texts have no 也 at all and write 常 for 恒. (Jicheng IV, p. 43, n. 2; p. 208, n. 1)
  • 无名萬物之始也: The manuscripts agree. Wang Bi reads 無名，天地之始 ("the nameless, the beginning of heaven and earth"); both silk manuscripts have 萬物 "the ten thousand things" in this clause, matching the next line, 有名萬物之母也.
  • 故恒无欲也: Follows MS A; MS B agrees. The 故 stands at the foot of MS A's col. 93, with only its upper strokes surviving; the Jicheng reads it directly. (MS A, col. 93; Jicheng IV, p. 40)
  • 以觀其眇: Follows MS A; the line is lost in MS B and is restored by the Jicheng as 以觀其眇. 眇 is read as 妙 "subtle", the graph of Wang Bi.
  • 恒有欲也: Follows MS A; MS B reads 恆又欲也, with 又 for 有.
  • 以觀其所噭: Follows MS A; MS B reads 以觀亓所噭. In the Dao section MS A writes 其, MS B 亓, and so throughout. The Jicheng reads 噭 as 徼 "bounds". Wang Bi has 以觀其徼, without 所.
  • 兩者同出 / 異名同胃: The manuscripts agree; 胃 is read as 謂. Wang Bi reads 此兩者，同出而異名，同謂之玄; the silk text has neither 此 nor the 之玄 after 同謂, and so punctuates differently.
  • 玄之有玄: Follows MS A, with 有 read as 又 "again"; MS B reads 玄之又玄, as does Wang Bi.
  • 眾眇之門: The manuscripts agree. In MS A the 門 at the foot of col. 94 is read directly by the Jicheng. Wang Bi has 眾妙之門. (MS A, col. 94)

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The Dao's being in all-under-heaven is like the valley-streams' being to the rivers and seas — formless, featureless, and the ten thousand things owe it their lives. The crux of the chapter: the parting of Dao and name, the dividing of presence and absence, and the point of the two beholdings. And the depth of it all shows in the variant graphs of the silk text and the Wang Bi recension.

"The Dao that can be spoken of is not the constant Dao" — what can be spoken is not the ever-enduring Dao. "The name that can be named is not the constant name" — what can be named is not the ever-enduring name. This is Laozi warning himself: the names used in these five thousand words are not constant names either — reader, do not take my words for the Dao. This chapter's head is the gate for reading the book, not the substance of the Dao itself.

Now set the silk text against the Wang Bi recension, and three differences stand out. First: the silk text writes 恒, "constant," where the Wang Bi recension writes 常 — a change traditionally explained by the naming taboo of Emperor Wen of Han. I draw on the graphs' later associations: 恒 evokes the enduring constancy of the Way of Heaven; 常 may call to mind the everyday affairs of men. A reader may pass from cosmic constancy to familiar experience. This is a reflection drawn from the graphs, not a claim that 常 has lost the sense of "constant" in this chapter. Second: the silk text's 亓 (manuscript B; A writes 其) for the Wang Bi recension's 其 — an old graph against the new, no more. Third: the silk text has "the nameless is the beginning of the ten thousand things; the named is the mother of the ten thousand things." The Wang Bi recension reads "the nameless is the beginning of heaven and earth; the named is the mother of the ten thousand things": 天地, "heaven and earth," stands where the silk text has 萬物, "the ten thousand things," in the first clause. With the silk text's pair, nameless-as-beginning and named-as-mother face each other, and the order stands clean: the nameless is the source, the named is what things issue from. The Wang Bi recension speaks first of heaven and earth, then of the ten thousand things. I favor the silk text's repeated "ten thousand things" for its clearer parallel.

As for the work of beholding the Dao: "constantly without desire, to behold its subtlety" — desire not yet risen, the heart is empty; empty, it is clear; clear, it can behold the Dao's subtlety. "Constantly with desire, to behold where it runs to its bounds" — with desire, one can know where things return to, the place they end. So without desire, behold the subtle and see the Dao's essence; with desire, behold the bounds and know where things come home. "These two issue from the same source; different names, one calling" — beholding-the-subtle and beholding-the-bounds: two names, one spring. "Dark and dark again — the gate of all subtleties": the Dao, dim and dimmer, deep and deeper, and the ten thousand presences and subtleties all come out of it; embody the dark and enter, and subtleties stand thick on every side — everything you touch is the Dao. Not two darknesses: the dark, deepened and deepened again — to say the Dao's body cannot be fathomed to its floor.

Yet Laozi's whole point comes to one sentence: this book is not the Dao. Read it as the Dao, and you are farther from the Dao. See it as the finger pointing at the moon — then you may look at the moon. Ah — the Dao is before your eyes and the lost walk away from it; names rise with desire and the Dao hides in absence. Let the student leave the highway of names and words and come home to the nameless beginning — and he is nearly there.
