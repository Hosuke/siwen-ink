The Book of De · Chapter Thirty-eight · Heaven's Net

English Translation

He who is brave in daring gets killed; he who is brave in not-daring stays whole (栝). […] two: one profits, one harms.

What Heaven hates — who knows the reason?

The Way of Heaven: it strikes not (不單), yet wins well; speaks not, yet answers well; uncalled, comes of itself; at ease (彈 — read here as 繟然), yet plans well.

Heaven's net is vast (𮵚𮵚, read 恢恢); its meshes are wide — and nothing slips through.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

Manuscript A (MS A) is badly damaged in this chapter: only 不言而善應, 不召而自來 and 彈而善謀 follow MS A; everything else follows Manuscript B (MS B), and □兩者或利或害 is damaged in both. Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 男於敢則殺: Follows MS B, which writes 男 for 勇 "brave" throughout. MS A opens the chapter with a division dot and reads 勇於敢者□殺, with a 者 after 敢 that is absent from MS B, the Peking University manuscript and the received texts. Its 殺 was a restoration in the 1980 edition; the Jicheng reads it directly from a fragment newly joined by Chen Jian, which had been mounted upside down. Wang Bi reads 勇於敢則殺. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 179)
  • 男於不敢則栝: Follows MS B. MS A reads □於不敢者則栝, likewise with 者. In MS B the right side of 栝 is damaged; the original editors fixed the graph on the ground that 活 is always written with 木 in the silk manuscripts. 栝 is read as 活 "to live"; Wang Bi reads 勇於不敢則活. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 179; p. 204, n. 138)
  • □兩者或利或害: Damaged in both manuscripts. MS B has lost its first graph. In MS A the whole line is lost: the original editors restored 知此兩者, but since the Peking University manuscript and most received texts lack 知, and for MS B the editors had restored only 此, the Jicheng drops 知 and prints 【此】兩者. Wang Bi reads 此兩者，或利或害. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 180)
  • 天之所亞 / 孰知亓故: Follows MS B; 亞 is read as 惡 "to hate". MS A's 知亓故 was part of the 1980 edition's restoration; the Jicheng reads it directly from a newly joined fragment. Wang Bi then adds 是以聖人猶難之 ("hence even the sage finds it hard"), which neither manuscript has. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 181)
  • 天之道: Follows MS B; MS A's 天之 is also read from the newly joined fragment, and its 道 is lost. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 181)
  • 不單而善朕: Follows MS B; 單 is read as 戰 "to do battle", and 朕 as 勝 "to win". MS A preserves only 善, a restoration in the 1980 edition, now read directly by the Jicheng from the newly joined fragment. Wang Bi reads 不爭而善勝. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 182)
  • 不言而善應: Follows MS A; both manuscripts agree. For MS A's 應 the Jicheng prints a form with 疒 enclosing 應, read as 應.
  • 不召而自來: Follows MS A; MS B writes 弗 for 不.
  • 彈而善謀: Follows MS A; MS B reads 單而善謀, Wang Bi 繟然而善謀.
  • 天罔𮵚𮵚: Follows MS B, which writes 天罔𮵚﹦ with a repetition mark after 𮵚. The 1980 edition and Gao Ming transcribe the graph with 示 beside 圣, the Jicheng as 経 with 糸; following the plate, it is given here as 𮵚, read as 恢 "vast". MS A reads 天罔恢□: 天罔恢 was part of the 1980 restoration, and the Jicheng reads it directly from a new join of three small fragments; the repetition mark after 恢 is lost. 罔 is read as 網 "net"; Wang Bi reads 天網恢恢. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 184)
  • 疏而不失: Follows MS B; MS A preserves only 疏而, also on the newly joined fragment. (Jicheng IV, p. 36, n. 184)

Commentary

Courage, for men, divides by its use. He who is brave in daring is resolute in contrivance, a strong-arm walking into peril — that is what gets him killed. He who is brave in not-daring has courage and reins it in, reading the hour and keeping his lot — that is what keeps him alive. It is not that Laozi holds courage cheap; he holds dear using it aright. As the saying runs, "without speaking, good at answering": heaven does not speak, and the four seasons proceed; a man who does not speak may yet answer all-under-heaven. The way of using courage is the same.

"Of these two, one profits, one harms" (manuscript B, its first graph damaged — Wang Bi has 此, "these"; manuscript A is lost here) — the same courage, profit or harm by how it is used. No fixed rule: it shifts with time and circumstance. This is Laozi's dialectic.

"What Heaven hates — who knows the reason?" Heaven's likings cannot be sounded by human wit. Even the sage keeps his awe and does not presume to guess Heaven's heart. Hence the four images of the Way of Heaven that follow. Without battle, good at winning: manuscript B writes 不單而善朕, 單 read as 戰 and 朕 as 勝 — the Way of Heaven joins no battle, and things submit of themselves; set against Wang Bi's 不爭, "not contending," the silk wording is plainer and more concrete, and the senses answer each other. Without speaking, good at answering: no injunction given, and the answer arrives of itself. Unsummoned, it comes of itself: the Way of Heaven transforms by drawing, with no need of calling — things come submitting of themselves. And "unhurried, good at planning" (彈而善謀 in manuscript A; manuscript B writes 單): Heaven dwells at ease and plans things well. Wang Bi writes 繟然而善謀 — 繟 is slack and slow — close in sense.

"Heaven's net is vast; its meshes wide, and nothing slips through" (manuscript B writes 天罔𮵚𮵚 — 𮵚, 示 beside 圣, read as 恢; manuscript A reads 天罔恢□, its 天罔恢 recovered by the Jicheng from a newly joined fragment, the repetition mark lost): 恢恢 is Heaven's measure — its span is the widest; 疏 is the wideness of its meshes — and yet the requital of good and evil follows like a shadow, and no one walks free by luck. The world is dazzled by what daring can do and blind to what not-daring profits, not knowing that the Way of Heaven lies not in fineness but in certainty: it does not set men free for want of watching, and none escapes for want of form. As the saying runs, "only by not contending is one without fault" — he who knows this can keep his life whole, can pattern on Heaven. The differences between the two texts come to two points: Wang Bi adds the line "hence even the sage treats it as hard," showing the sage's heart keeps its awe always; and 不單 against 不爭, 彈而 against 繟然而 — the graphs differ in age, the senses run together. Set side by side, the silk text is spare of words and whole in meaning — not something later hands could have padded.
