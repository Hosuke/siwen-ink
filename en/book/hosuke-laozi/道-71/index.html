The Book of Dao · Chapter Twenty-seven · The Drawing Forth of Light

English Translation

Good walkers leave no track. Good speakers leave no flaw to find. Good reckoners use no counting-rods. Good closers use no bolt or bar — yet none can open. Good binders use no cord or knot — yet none can untie.

Hence the sage is constantly good at saving people, and no one is thrown away; no thing, no goods are thrown away. This is called the drawing forth of light (忡明; MS B writes 曳明).

So: the good are the teachers of the good; the not-good are the good men's resource (齎).

Not prizing one's teacher, not loving one's resource — though knowing, one is greatly blind (大眯). This is called the subtle hinge (眇要).

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The text follows Manuscript A (MS A); 善言者无瑕適, 善結者无𦄿約而不可解也 and 故善人 / 善人之師, damaged in MS A, are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 善行者无勶迹: Follows MS A, with 勶 read as 轍 "wheel-rut"; MS B reads 善行者无達迹. Wang Bi has 善行無轍迹, without 者, as in the next four lines.
  • 善言者无瑕適: In MS A 善 is lost; the line follows MS B. 適 is read as 謫 "fault". Wang Bi has 善言無瑕讁.
  • 善數者不以檮𥮥: Follows MS A. 檮 is read as 籌 "tally"; 𥮥, 竹 over 析, as the plates, the 1980 edition and Gao Ming's transcription agree, is read as 策 "counting-rod". MS B reads 善數者不用檮𫁶, with 用 for 以; its graph for 策 was transcribed in 1980 as 竹 over 尺, and the Jicheng re-transcribes it as 竹 over 片, the 片 element being the ideographic original of 析 "to split". Wang Bi has 善數不用籌策. (Jicheng IV, p. 209, n. 42)
  • 善閉者无𫔢籥而不可啟也: Follows MS A. 𫔢, 門 pierced by a vertical stroke, is read as 關 "bar", and 籥 as 闔 "door-leaf". MS B writes 關籥; after its 善 the scribe first miswrote 數, which is circled for deletion. Wang Bi has 善閉無關楗而不可開. (Jicheng IV, p. 210, n. 43)
  • 善結者无𦄿約而不可解也: In MS A 无纆 is lost; the line follows MS B, whose 纆 "cord" is written with 糸 beside 黑, a variant of 纆. Wang Bi has 善結無繩約而不可解. (Jicheng IV, p. 52, n. 106)
  • 是以聲人恒善㤹人: Follows MS A, with 聲 read as 聖 "sage"; MS B reads 是以𦔻人恆善㤹人. 㤹 is read as 救 "to save". In MS A its right side is clearly 求, while the curving stroke at lower left could be either 心 or 辶; the 1980 edition and Gao Ming's transcription both take it as 忄. Wang Bi has 是以聖人常善救人.
  • 物无棄財是胃忡明: Follows MS A. The Jicheng takes MS A's 忡 as a scribal error for a graph composed of 忄 and 曳 and marks the correction in angle brackets; MS B writes 曳, which the Jicheng likewise reads as that 忄-曳 graph. Wang Bi has 常善救物，故無棄物，是謂襲明, with an extra line on saving things, 物 for 財 "goods", and 襲 for 曳.
  • 故善人 / 善人之師: In MS A 故善 carries a repetition mark, but 人 and its mark are lost and restored by the Jicheng as 故善﹦【人﹦】; the text follows MS B's 故善﹦人﹦之師, read as 故善人，善人之師 "the good man is the teacher of the good man". Wang Bi has 故善人者，不善人之師 "the good man is the teacher of the man who is not good": neither silk manuscript has 不, and the sense differs.
  • 善人之齎也: Follows MS A, with 齎 read as 資 "resource"; MS B reads 善人之資也. Wang Bi has 善人之資.
  • 不愛其齎唯知乎大眯: Follows MS A, whose 愛 is a variant of 㤅 corrected by the Jicheng to 〈愛〉; 唯 is read as 雖 "although", 知 as 智 "clever", and 眯 as 迷 "astray". MS B reads 不愛亓資雖知乎大迷. Wang Bi has 不愛其資，雖智大迷.
  • 是胃眇要: The manuscripts agree; Wang Bi has 是謂要妙, with the two graphs reversed and 妙 for 眇.

Commentary

The point of this chapter: the sage's goodness has no fixed method, and the subtlety of the Dao lies in the two words "without using." The differences between silk text and Wang Bi here are not mere letters — they are differences in the depth of the doctrine.

The five goods are walking, speaking, reckoning, closing, binding. "Good walkers leave no track" (善行者无勶迹, 勶 read as 轍) — the work done and not dwelt in. "Good speakers leave no flaw to find" (善言者无瑕適) — speech issued from the self-so of heaven's pattern, no crack to probe. "Good reckoners use no counting-rods" — heaven's pattern complete in one heart, no need of outer gear. "Good closers use no bolt or bar" (善閉者无𫔢籥) — no crack, so no lock installed. "Good binders use no cord or knot" (善結者无𦄿約, so manuscript B; 𦄿 is 纆) — held by the Dao-heart, nothing can scatter. Five different works, one home: "without using" — the self-so, leaning on nothing outside.

The one standing difference: manuscript B writes 曳明 (manuscript A writes 忡, which the Jicheng takes as an error for 㥏, with 忄 beside 曳) where Wang Bi writes 襲明. The original editors' note cites the Shuowen, "㥏 is to practice (習)," and the Cangjie pian, "㥏 is bright," and holds that 襲 and 習 were used interchangeably of old — so 曳 (㥏) 明 is 襲明, the graphs different, sound and sense connected. Yet I venture that manuscript B's 曳 may be read as written. 曳 is to draw and pull — the sage leads the ten thousand things forward, bringing each home to its own light. The Shuowen gives 曳 as drawing-onward: there is guiding-ahead in it. 襲 has the robe-sign — receiving and laying over: the sage inherits light from the Dao and shines it down on all-under-heaven. Read as written, the silk text's 曳 is the sage's own hand at the work of drawing — active; Wang Bi's 襲 is the sage draped in the light he has received — transforming by non-doing. The two senses foot each other, but 曳明 shows more of the sage's vast vow and deep heart. And "no thing, no goods thrown away" against the received "nothing thrown away": the silk text puts 財, goods, where the received text has 物, thing — not only is no person disposable, no thing and no goods are disposable either. This is how the sage is great.

As for "so: the good are the teachers of the good; the not-good are the good men's resource" (齎, read as 資): Wang Bi writes 故善人者，不善人之師, "the good man is the teacher of the not-good," but neither silk manuscript has 不, and the sense differs from the received text. The good are teachers to the good, so the teacher's way passes on among the good; the not-good are the good men's resource. Teacher and resource complete each other: prize the teacher and goodness grows daily; love the resource and faults shrink daily. But Laozi goes one layer deeper: "not prizing one's teacher, not loving one's resource — though knowing, one is greatly blind: this is called the subtle hinge." 唯 is read as 雖, "though"; 眯 is bewilderment; 眇 is the subtle; 要 is the hinge. Prizing the teacher swells one's pride; loving the resource discards men — neither reaches the dark sameness. Only the sage does not make himself the teacher nor the other the resource: teacher and resource both forgotten, and the Dao is whole. This is the subtle hinge.

The silk text writes 眇要 where Wang Bi writes 要妙 — 眇 is the deep-minute, 妙 the fine-subtle; the senses run together, but 眇要 holds both depth and urgency, pointing straight at the unnamable utmost of the Dao. "Of old, those good at working the Dao were minute and subtle, darkly far-reaching" — exactly this ground.

Ah — the use of the five goods lies in without-using; the subtlety of without-using lies in non-doing; the utmost of non-doing lies in drawing all-under-heaven home to the light. The sage draws things until they are light of themselves — he does not lay his own light on them — and so things come home to the Dao of themselves, and the sage has no merit in it. Everyone knows the good as good; no one knows the not-good is also the good men's resource — the law of the dark sameness, the same as water's benefiting the ten thousand things and keeping still. Let the student savor this until it yields, and the De of throwing away no one, throwing away nothing, can be had sitting down.
