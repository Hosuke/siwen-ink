The Book of Dao · Chapter Twenty-seven · The Drawing Forth of Light

English Translation

Good walkers leave no track. Good speakers leave no flaw to find. Good reckoners use no counting-rods. Good closers use no bolt or bar — yet none can open. Good binders use no cord or knot — yet none can untie.

Hence the sage is constantly good at saving people, and no one is thrown away; no thing, no goods are thrown away. This is called the drawing forth of light (曳明).

So: the good are the teachers of the good; the not-good are the good men's resource (齎).

Not prizing one's teacher, not loving one's resource — though knowing, one is greatly blind (大眯). This is called the subtle hinge (眇要).

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

The point of this chapter: the sage's goodness has no fixed method, and the subtlety of the Dao lies in the two words "without using." The differences between silk text and Wang Bi here are not mere letters — they are differences in the depth of the doctrine.

The five goods are walking, speaking, reckoning, closing, binding. "Good walkers leave no track" — the work done and not dwelt in. "Good speakers leave no flaw to find" — speech issued from the self-so of heaven's pattern, no crack to probe. "Good reckoners use no counting-rods" — heaven's pattern complete in one heart, no need of outer gear. "Good closers use no bolt" — no crack, so no lock installed. "Good binders use no cord" — held by the Dao-heart, nothing can scatter. Five different works, one home: "without using" — the self-so, leaning on nothing outside.

The one standing difference: the silk text writes 曳明 where Wang Bi writes 襲明 — one graph apart, and the senses are a sky apart. 曳 is to draw and pull — the sage leads the ten thousand things forward, bringing each home to its own light. The Shuowen gives 曳 as drawing-onward: there is guiding-ahead in it. 襲 is the robe laid over — receiving and covering: the sage inherits light from the Dao and shines it down on the world. The silk text's 曳 is the sage's own hand at the work of drawing — active; Wang Bi's 襲 is the sage draped in the light he has received — transforming by non-doing. The two senses foot each other, but 曳明 shows more of the sage's vast vow and deep heart. And "no thing, no goods thrown away" against the received "nothing thrown away": the silk text puts 財, goods, where the received text has 物, thing — not only is no person disposable, no thing and no goods are disposable either. This is how the sage is great.

As for "the good are the teachers of the good; the not-good are the good men's store" — one sense with the received texts. Teacher and resource complete each other: prize the teacher and goodness grows daily; love the resource and faults shrink daily. But Laozi goes one layer deeper: "not prizing one's teacher, not loving one's resource — though knowing, one is greatly blind: this is called the subtle hinge." 眯 is bewilderment; 眇 is the subtle; 要 is the hinge. Prizing the teacher swells one's pride; loving the resource discards men — neither reaches the dark sameness. Only the sage does not make himself the teacher nor the other the resource: teacher and resource both forgotten, and the Dao is whole. This is the subtle hinge.

The silk text writes 眇要 where Wang Bi writes 要妙 — 眇 is the deep-minute, 妙 the fine-subtle; the senses run together, but 眇要 holds both depth and urgency, pointing straight at the unnamable utmost of the Dao. "Of old, those good at working the Dao were minute and subtle, darkly far-reaching" — exactly this ground.

Ah — the use of the five goods lies in without-using; the subtlety of without-using lies in non-doing; the utmost of non-doing lies in drawing all-under-heaven home to the light. The sage draws things until they are light of themselves — he does not lay his own light on them — and so things come home to the Dao of themselves, and the sage has no merit in it. Everyone knows the good as good; no one knows the not-good is also the good men's store — the law of the dark sameness, the same as water's benefiting the ten thousand things and keeping still. Let the student savor this until it yields, and the De of throwing away no one, throwing away nothing, can be had sitting down.
