The Book of De · Chapter Twenty-three · Dwelling in the Seat

English Translation

Govern a great state as you would cook a small fish.

Preside over all-under-heaven by the Dao (道), and its ghosts have no uncanny power. Not that its ghosts lack the power — their power does not hurt men. Not only does their power not hurt men — the sage too does no hurt.

Neither of the two causes harm; so their De (德) flows together and comes home.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

Govern a great state as you would cook a small fish — the famous saying, spoken plain. A small fish's flesh is tenderest: stir it a few times and it falls to mush. A great state's people and affairs are thickest: multiply the business and they are thrown into turmoil. The sage knows this to the bottom, and so presides in clear stillness. The Analects says "govern by De — like the pole star": the same thought of not disturbing.

Preside over all-under-heaven by the Dao, and its ghosts have no uncanny power. When one dwells in the seat by the Dao and governs all-under-heaven, ghosts and spirits dare not hurt men — from which we know it is not that the ghosts have truly lost their power: when the Dao fills the land, the people no longer borrow ghosts and spirits for their blessings. The silk text writes the plain pair 亓鬼不神、亓神不傷人; both texts unfold the same three turns on two 非s: one thread of sense either way. In sum: where the Dao is present, the numinous does not hurt men, and the sage hurts no one either — high and low both rooted in the straight.

Neither of the two causes harm, so their De flows together and comes home. Ghosts and spirits do not hurt men; the sage does not hurt men; neither preys on the other; and the transforming De of it streams through all-under-heaven. The teaching of governing by non-doing has the sage trusting the self-so, walking the four seas without stepping from the hall: how does that differ from cooking the small fish? Both are not-disturbing. Today's governors do the opposite: cleverness to harry the people, awe and punishment to cow them, not knowing that yielding is De. The sage yields to the people's self-so; ghosts and spirits yield to men's self-mastery; high and low yield each to each and invade nothing — the utmost rule of a world in order.
