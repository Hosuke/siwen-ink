The Book of Dao · Chapter Two · Beholding the Bounds

English Translation

When all-under-heaven knows the beautiful as beautiful — that is ugly already. When all know the good as good — that is not-good already (訾, read here as 斯, "this").

For: presence (有) and absence (无) give birth to each other; hard and easy complete each other; long and short shape each other; high and low fill (盈) each other; voice and tone harmonize with each other; before and after follow each other — this is constant.

Hence the sage dwells in the work of non-doing (无為) and walks the teaching without words.

The ten thousand things arise, and he does not start them; he works, and does not lean on it; the work completes, and he does not dwell in it. Only because he does not dwell does it not depart.

Notes on Silk Manuscripts A and B

The chapter largely follows Manuscript A (MS A); the two lines 行不言之教 and 萬物昔而弗始 are damaged in MS A and are supplied from Manuscript B (MS B). Graphs are those outside the brackets in the Changsha Mawangdui Han mu jianbo jicheng (2014; hereafter Jicheng).

  • 天下皆知美 / 為美惡巳: Follows MS A, which reads 天下皆知美為美，惡巳, without 之; MS B reads 天下皆知美之為美，亞巳, with 之 and with 亞 for 惡 "ugly". The Jicheng reads 巳 as 已 "already". Wang Bi has 天下皆知美之為美，斯惡已.
  • 訾不善矣: Follows MS A; the Jicheng reads 訾 as 斯 "this". MS B reads 斯不善矣. Wang Bi has 斯不善已.
  • 有无之相生也: Follows MS A; in MS B the four graphs 有無之相 are lost. Wang Bi has 故有無相生, without 之 or 也; in the silk text this and the five lines that follow all carry 之 and 也, which Wang Bi everywhere lacks.
  • 長短之相刑也: The manuscripts agree; 刑 is read as 形 "to shape". Wang Bi has 長短相較 "long and short measure against each other".
  • 高下之相盈也: The manuscripts agree; Wang Bi has 高下相傾 "high and low lean on each other".
  • 意聲之相和也: Follows MS A, which writes 意; the Jicheng treats it as a miswriting of 音 "tone" and prints 意〈音〉. MS B writes 音, as does Wang Bi.
  • 先後之相隋: The manuscripts agree; 隋 is read as 隨 "to follow". Wang Bi has 前後相隨.
  • 恒也是以聲人居无為之事: Follows MS A. Both manuscripts have 恒也 "this is constant" after 相隋; Wang Bi lacks it. MS A's 聲人 is read by the Jicheng as 聖人 "the sage"; MS B writes 𦔻 (耳 beside 口). Wang Bi has 是以聖人處無為之事.
  • 行不言之教: MS A preserves only 行; the rest is supplied from MS B.
  • 萬物昔而弗始: Follows MS B, whose 昔 is read as 作 "to arise". The line is broken in MS A, which the Jicheng restores as 萬物作而弗□也. Of the graph before 也 only a stroke at its lower right survives; the 1980 edition treated it as a lacuna and supplied 始 from MS B, whereas the Jicheng marks it ☐ with 始 in parentheses. Wang Bi reads 萬物作焉而不辭，生而不有; the clause 生而不有 "gives life and does not possess" is absent from both silk manuscripts. (Jicheng IV, p. 44, n. 12)
  • 為而弗志也: Follows MS A; 志 is read as 恃 "to rely on". MS B reads 為而弗侍也, its 侍 also read as 恃. Wang Bi has 為而不恃.
  • 成功而弗居也: The manuscripts agree; Wang Bi has 功成而弗居.
  • 夫唯居: Follows MS A. The 1980 editors noted that all other texts read 夫唯弗居 and that MS A has dropped a 弗; the Jicheng supplies 弗 in omission brackets, the graph being absent from the manuscript itself. MS B reads 夫唯弗居, as does Wang Bi. (Jicheng IV, p. 45, n. 14)
  • 是以弗去: The manuscripts agree; Wang Bi has 是以不去.

Commentary

Yaomiao says:

"When all-under-heaven knows the beautiful as beautiful — that is ugly already; when all know the good as good — that is not-good already." The name "beautiful" once set up, the ugly follows on its heels; the name "good" once established, the not-good rises. For once the name is fixed, the complement forms against it: the opposite is born at the very moment the name stands — there is no "ugly" somewhere outside the beautiful. So with the six pairs that follow: presence and absence, hard and easy, long and short, high and low, voice and tone, before and after — six mutual dependencies, contraries that complete each other; not two things at war, but the circulation of one qi. The Book of Changes says, "one yin and one yang: that is called the Dao" — is it not this?

After "before and after follow each other" (先後之相隋, 隋 read as 隨), the silk text carries the two graphs 恒也, "constantly," which Wang Bi cut. The silk text runs deeper: this following is no accident of the moment — it is the constant law of the Dao's own substance. Laozi sets the graph 恒 like a seal on the point, lest we take mutual dependence for a passing sight: it is heaven and earth's unchanging rule. And the silk text's 相盈, "fill each other," against Wang Bi's 相傾, "tip into each other": 盈 is brimming fulfillment — high and low make each other full; 傾 is toppling pressure — high and low prey on each other. One graph, and the whole chapter's temper hangs on it; it must be weighed.

"Hence the sage dwells in the work of non-doing and walks the teaching without words." The six pairs clear, the sage's way of handling things differs from the world's. He takes neither beautiful-and-ugly nor good-and-not-good for his plumb line — only non-doing and wordlessness. Dwelling in non-doing, he does not set up the name of doing and summon not-doing's opposite; walking wordlessness, he does not lay down tracks of verbal teaching and summon wordlessness's counterpart. Let a name stand, and its complement is born; the complement born, the trouble arrives. This is why the sage does not dwell among names, and so joins the true of the Dao.

"The ten thousand things arise, and he does not start them (manuscript B; 昔, read as 作); he works, and does not lean on it (志, read as 恃); the work completes, and he does not dwell in it." Arising — he lets them be self-so, and does not make himself their starter; working — he transforms and completes, without self-reliance; completing — the work done among the ten thousand things, he takes no seat in it. Three not-dwellings — and so the merit stays and does not depart. The Wang Bi recension reads "the ten thousand things arise, and he does not decline them," and carries one clause more than the silk text: "he gives them life, and does not possess them."

"Only because he does not dwell does it not depart" (manuscript A drops the 弗, "not," of "does not dwell"; restored from manuscript B). To take one's seat is to lose it; to take no seat is to keep it forever. Completing by not-completing, gaining by not-gaining — all the Dao's turning-back. The sage watches the emptiness of mutual dependence, knows names and words for phantoms, keeps the Dao and not the name — so the ten thousand things transform of themselves, and work and name do not depart. Let the student wake to this, and he will see that the good-and-evil, beautiful-and-ugly he sets up are opposites of his own making — not that the world is without good and evil, but that unless you grip this good, that evil never forms. The sage keeps the Dao and not the name — is this not what we call dark De?
